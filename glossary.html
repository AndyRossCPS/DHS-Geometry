Glossary

30-60-90 triangle

A right triangle with angles of \(30^\circ\), \(60^\circ\), and \(90^\circ\). If the short leg is \(x\), the long leg is \(x\sqrt{3}\) and the hypotenuse is \(2x\).

Example: Short leg \(4\), long leg \(4\sqrt{3}\), hypotenuse \(8\).

See Also: special right triangle, equilateral triangle

45-45-90 triangle

A right triangle with two \(45^\circ\) angles. Its legs are equal, and the hypotenuse is the leg times \(\sqrt{2}\).

Example: Legs \(5\) and \(5\) give a hypotenuse of \(5\sqrt{2}\).

See Also: special right triangle, hypotenuse

AA similarity

If two angles of one triangle are equal to two angles of another triangle, the triangles are similar.

Example: Two triangles each have angles of \(50^\circ\) and \(60^\circ\). They are similar by AA.

See Also: similar

AAS

Angle, angle, side. If two angles and a side that is not between them in one triangle are congruent to the matching parts of another triangle, the triangles are congruent.

acute angle

An angle that measures more than \(0^\circ\) and less than \(90^\circ\).

See Also: right angle, obtuse angle

adjacent angles

Two angles that share a vertex and one side, and do not overlap.

See Also: linear pair

adjacent side

In a right triangle, the leg that touches a chosen acute angle. (The hypotenuse touches it too, but it is not called adjacent.)

Example: In \(\triangle ABC\) with the right angle at \(C\), side \(AC\) is adjacent to \(\angle A\).

See Also: opposite side, cosine

alternate exterior angles

Two angles outside the two lines, on opposite sides of the transversal. If the two lines are parallel, alternate exterior angles are equal.

See Also: alternate interior angles

alternate interior angles

Two angles between the two lines, on opposite sides of the transversal. If the two lines are parallel, alternate interior angles are equal.

See Also: alternate exterior angles, same-side interior angles

angle

Two rays with the same endpoint. The endpoint is the vertex. Write \(\angle ABC\), with the vertex in the middle.

Example:

\(\angle ABC\), \(\angle B\), and \(\angle 1\) can all name the same angle.

See Also: vertex, degree

angle bisector

A ray that starts at the vertex of an angle and cuts the angle into two congruent angles.

Example:

If \(\overrightarrow{BD}\) bisects \(\angle ABC\) and \(m\angle ABC = 68^\circ\), then \(m\angle ABD = 34^\circ\).

See Also: congruent

angle of depression

The angle from the horizontal down to a line of sight. It equals the angle of elevation from the lower point to the higher point.

Example: A lifeguard in a tall chair looks down at a swimmer at an angle of depression of \(20^\circ\).

See Also: angle of elevation, alternate interior angles

angle of elevation

The angle from the horizontal up to a line of sight.

Example: A student looks up at the top of a tower. The angle between the horizontal and the line of sight is the angle of elevation.

See Also: angle of depression

angle of rotation

The amount a figure turns in a rotation, in degrees. Counterclockwise turns are positive.

Example:

A turn of \(90^\circ\) clockwise has the same result as an angle of rotation of \(270^\circ\) counterclockwise.

See Also: rotation

annulus

The ring-shaped region between two circles with the same center. Its area is \(\pi R^2 - \pi r^2\), where \(R\) is the outer radius and \(r\) is the inner radius.

Example: Outer radius \(10\) and inner radius \(6\): \(100\pi - 36\pi = 64\pi\).

See Also: area, shaded region

apothem

The distance from the center of a regular polygon to the middle of a side. It is perpendicular to that side.

Example: A regular hexagon with side 6 has an apothem of \(3\sqrt{3}\).

See Also: regular polygon, area

arc

Part of a circle between two points on the circle. Two points split a circle into a minor arc and a major arc (or two semicircles).

Example: In circle \(P\), the arc from \(A\) to \(D\) is written \(\overset{\frown}{AD}\).

See Also: minor arc, major arc, semicircle

arc length

The distance along an arc. It is a length, in units such as inches or meters. Arc length \(= \dfrac{\text{central angle}}{360^\circ} \cdot 2\pi r\).

Example: A \(90^\circ\) arc on a circle with radius \(4\) is \(\dfrac{90}{360} \cdot 2\pi(4) = 2\pi\) units long.

See Also: arc measure, circumference

arc measure

The size of an arc in degrees. It equals the measure of its central angle. A semicircle measures \(180^\circ\). A circle measures \(360^\circ\) all the way around.

Example: A central angle of \(70^\circ\) cuts off an arc that measures \(70^\circ\).

See Also: arc length, central angle

area

The number of square units inside a figure.

Example: A rectangle 6 by 4 has area \(24\) square units.

See Also: perimeter

ASA

Angle, side, angle. If two angles and the included side of one triangle are congruent to two angles and the included side of another triangle, the triangles are congruent.

See Also: included side

axis of revolution

The line that a flat shape spins around to make a solid of revolution.

See Also: solid of revolution

base angles

The two angles of an isosceles triangle that touch the base. Base angles are congruent.

Example:

If the vertex angle is \(40^\circ\), each base angle is \(70^\circ\).

base of a polygon

The side of a parallelogram, triangle, or trapezoid that you measure the height to. You can choose any side as the base.

See Also: height of a polygon

base of an isosceles triangle

The side of an isosceles triangle that is not one of the two congruent sides.

Example:

In \(\triangle ABC\) with \(AB \cong AC\), the base is \(\overline{BC}\).

bounding box

The smallest rectangle with sides on grid lines that has a figure inside it. It is used to find area by subtracting the corner pieces.

See Also: area

Cavalieri’s principle

If two solids have the same height and the same cross-section area at every level, they have the same volume.

Example: A slanted stack of coins has the same volume as a straight stack of the same coins.

See Also: volume, cross section

center of a circle

The point inside a circle that is the same distance from every point on the circle. We name a circle by its center.

Example: In \(\odot P\), the center is point \(P\).

See Also: circle, radius

center of dilation

The fixed point a dilation moves points toward or away from. Each point and its image are on a ray from the center.

Example:

A projector is like a center of dilation. The slide is small and the picture on the wall is large.

See Also: dilation

center of rotation

The fixed point a figure turns about in a rotation.

Example:

A Ferris wheel turns about its center.

See Also: rotation

central angle

An angle whose vertex is the center of a circle. Its sides are two radii.

Example: \(\angle APD\) in circle \(P\) is a central angle.

See Also: arc measure, radius

chord

A segment with both endpoints on a circle. A diameter is a chord that goes through the center.

Example: In circle \(P\), \(\overline{EF}\) is a chord.

See Also: diameter, circle

circle

All the points in a plane that are the same distance from one point, the center. We write circle \(P\) as \(\odot P\).

Example: A bike wheel is a circle with its center at the hub.

See Also: center of a circle, radius

circumference

The distance around a circle. \(C = \pi d = 2\pi r\).

Example: A circle with \(r = 5\) has \(C = 2\pi(5) = 10\pi \approx 31.42\).

See Also: pi, diameter

collinear

Points that lie on the same line.

See Also: coplanar

compass

A tool that draws arcs and circles. The width of the compass stays the same while you draw, so every point on the arc is the same distance from the center.

complementary

Two angles whose measures add to \(90^\circ\). Each angle is the complement of the other.

Example:

\(25^\circ\) and \(65^\circ\) are complementary because \(25 + 65 = 90\).

See Also: supplementary

completing the square

A way to rewrite \(x^2 + bx\) as a perfect square by adding \(\left(\dfrac{b}{2}\right)^2\). Add the same number to both sides of an equation.

Example: \(x^2 - 6x + 9 = (x - 3)^2\).

See Also: perfect square

composite figure

A figure made of two or more simple shapes. Find its area by adding the areas of the parts, or by subtracting a part from a larger shape.

Example: An L-shaped room is a large rectangle with a small rectangle removed.

See Also: shaded region, area

cone

A solid with one circle as its base and a curved surface that comes to a point, the apex.

Example: A party hat is a cone.

See Also: pyramid, cylinder

congruence statement

A statement such as \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle FDE\). The order of the letters shows which parts correspond: \(A\) matches \(F\), \(B\) matches \(D\), and \(C\) matches \(E\).

See Also: congruent, corresponding parts

congruent

Having the same size and shape. Congruent segments have the same length. Congruent angles have the same measure. The symbol is \(\cong\).

Example:

If \(AB = 5\) and \(CD = 5\), then \(\overline{AB} \cong \overline{CD}\).

consecutive angles

Two angles of a polygon that share a side. In a parallelogram, consecutive angles are supplementary.

Example:

In parallelogram \(ABCD\), \(\angle A\) and \(\angle B\) are consecutive angles.

construction

A drawing made with only a compass and a straightedge. A construction uses no ruler and no protractor.

See Also: compass, straightedge

converse

The statement you get by turning an “if, then” rule around.

Example: The converse of the Pythagorean theorem: if \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\), the triangle is a right triangle.

See Also: Pythagorean theorem

convex polygon

A polygon where every segment between two points of the polygon stays inside it. A polygon that is not convex is concave. A concave polygon has a corner that points inward.

See Also: polygon

coordinate plane

A flat surface with a horizontal \(x\)-axis and a vertical \(y\)-axis. Every point has an ordered pair \((x, y)\).

coordinate proof

A way to show a fact for sure by using coordinates, slopes, and distances instead of a picture. Also called computation as proof.

Example: Two sides have slopes \(\dfrac{1}{3}\) and \(-3\). The product is \(-1\), so the sides are perpendicular.

coplanar

Points or lines that lie in the same plane.

See Also: collinear

corresponding angles

Two angles in the same position at the two places where a transversal crosses two lines. If the two lines are parallel, corresponding angles are equal.

See Also: transversal, alternate interior angles

corresponding parts

The sides and angles that match when one figure is moved onto a congruent figure. Corresponding parts of congruent figures are congruent.

Example:

If \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle FDE\), then \(\overline{AB}\) and \(\overline{FD}\) are corresponding sides, and \(\angle A\) and \(\angle F\) are corresponding angles.

See Also: congruence statement

cosine

For an acute angle \(A\) in a right triangle, \(\cos A = \dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\).

Example: \(\cos A = \dfrac{15}{17}\) when the adjacent side is \(15\) and the hypotenuse is \(17\).

See Also: sine, tangent

counterexample

One example that shows a statement is not always true.

Example:

Two triangles can have two sides and a non-included angle congruent without being congruent. This is a counterexample for SSA.

cross products

In the proportion \(\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{c}{d}\), the products \(a \cdot d\) and \(b \cdot c\). They are equal.

Example: In \(\dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{8}{12}\), the cross products are \(2 \cdot 12 = 24\) and \(3 \cdot 8 = 24\).

See Also: proportion

cross section

The flat shape you see when a plane slices through a solid.

Example: A horizontal slice of a cylinder is a circle.

See Also: solid, plane

cubic unit

A cube with edges of length \(1\) unit. Volume counts how many cubic units fill a solid. Examples are cubic inches, cubic feet, and cubic yards.

See Also: volume

cylinder

A solid with two parallel circles as bases and one curved surface between them.

Example: A soup can is a cylinder.

See Also: prism, cone

degree

The unit for measuring angles, written \(^\circ\). A full turn is \(360^\circ\). A right angle is \(90^\circ\).

density

How much mass is packed into each unit of volume: \(\text{density} = \dfrac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}\). Weight is found by multiplying density by volume.

Example: Water has a density of about \(62.4\) pounds per cubic foot. An object floats in water if its density is less than that.

See Also: volume

diagonal

A segment that joins two corners of a polygon that are not next to each other.

Example: A rectangle has two diagonals, and they have the same length.

diameter

A chord that goes through the center of a circle. Its length is twice the radius: \(d = 2r\).

Example: If \(r = 7\), then \(d = 14\).

See Also: radius, chord

dilation

A transformation that makes a figure larger or smaller from a fixed point, the center of dilation. It keeps the shape and angles but multiplies every length by the scale factor.

Example:

A dilation about the origin with scale factor 3 sends \((2, 1)\) to \((6, 3)\).

See Also: scale factor, center of dilation

distance formula

The distance between \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\) is \(d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}\).

Example: From \((0, 0)\) to \((3, 4)\): \(d = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5\).

See Also: Pythagorean theorem

edge

A segment where two faces of a polyhedron meet. A cube has 12 edges.

See Also: face, vertex of a solid

endpoint

A point at the end of a segment or the start of a ray.

enlargement

A dilation with a scale factor greater than 1. The image is larger than the original.

Example:

A scale factor of 2 makes an enlargement.

See Also: reduction

equation of a circle

The equation \((x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2\) describes a circle with center \((h, k)\) and radius \(r\).

Example: \((x - 2)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 9\) is a circle with center \((2, -1)\) and radius \(3\).

See Also: distance formula, radius

equiangular

Having all angles the same measure. An equiangular triangle has three \(60^\circ\) angles.

equilateral triangle

A triangle with all three sides the same length. Each angle measures \(60^\circ\).

See Also: equiangular

Euler’s formula

For a polyhedron with no holes, \(F + V - E = 2\). \(F\) is the number of faces, \(V\) the number of vertices, and \(E\) the number of edges.

Example: A cube has \(6 + 8 - 12 = 2\).

See Also: face, edge

exterior angle

An angle outside a polygon, made by one side and the extension of the next side. An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two remote interior angles.

Example:

If two angles of a triangle are \(50^\circ\) and \(65^\circ\), the exterior angle at the third vertex is \(115^\circ\).

See Also: linear pair

face

A flat surface of a polyhedron. A cube has 6 faces.

See Also: edge, polyhedron

frustum

A cone or pyramid with the top cut off by a plane parallel to the base. Find its volume by subtracting the small cone (or pyramid) from the large one.

Example: A flower pot and a funnel are frustums.

See Also: cone, pyramid, cross section

height of a polygon

The distance from the base to the opposite side (or opposite vertex), measured at a right angle to the base. It is not the length of a slanted side.

Example: A parallelogram with base 14 ft and a slanted side of 10 ft can have a height of 9 ft.

See Also: base of a polygon, area

hemisphere

Half of a sphere. Its volume is \(\frac{2}{3}\pi r^3\).

Example: A round bowl is close to a hemisphere.

See Also: sphere

HL

Hypotenuse, leg. If the hypotenuse and a leg of one right triangle are congruent to the hypotenuse and a leg of another right triangle, the triangles are congruent. HL works for right triangles only.

See Also: hypotenuse, leg

hypotenuse

The side of a right triangle across from the right angle. It is the longest side.

See Also: leg

image

The new figure after a transformation. The image of point \(A\) is named \(A'\) and is read “A prime”.

Example:

If \(A(1, 2)\) is slid 3 right, then \(A'\) is \((4, 2)\).

See Also: preimage

included angle

The angle between two sides of a triangle. It is formed by those two sides.

Example:

In \(\triangle ABC\), \(\angle B\) is the included angle of \(\overline{AB}\) and \(\overline{BC}\).

included side

The side between two angles of a triangle. It connects the vertices of those two angles.

Example:

In \(\triangle ABC\), \(\overline{AB}\) is the included side of \(\angle A\) and \(\angle B\).

indirect measurement

Finding a length that is hard to measure by using similar triangles and lengths that are easy to measure.

Example: A 5 ft person has an 8 ft shadow. A tree has a 40 ft shadow. The tree is \(\dfrac{5}{8} \cdot 40 = 25\) ft tall.

See Also: similar, proportion

inscribed angle

An angle with its vertex on a circle and both sides chords of the circle. Its measure is half of its intercepted arc.

Example: An inscribed angle that intercepts an \(80^\circ\) arc measures \(40^\circ\).

See Also: intercepted arc, central angle

inscribed quadrilateral

A four-sided figure with all four vertices on a circle. Its opposite angles are supplementary.

Example: If \(m\angle A = 70^\circ\), then the opposite angle is \(m\angle C = 110^\circ\).

See Also: inscribed angle, supplementary

intercepted arc

The arc that lies inside an inscribed or central angle, between its two sides.

Example: \(\angle ABC\) intercepts \(\overset{\frown}{AC}\).

See Also: inscribed angle, arc measure

intersect

To cross or meet. Two lines intersect at a point. Two planes intersect in a line.

inverse cosine

The angle whose cosine is a given number. Write \(\cos^{-1}(x)\). The \(-1\) is not an exponent.

Example: \(\cos^{-1}(0.5) = 60^\circ\)

See Also: cosine, inverse sine

inverse sine

The angle whose sine is a given number. Write \(\sin^{-1}(x)\). The \(-1\) is not an exponent.

Example: \(\sin^{-1}(0.5) = 30^\circ\)

See Also: sine, inverse cosine

inverse tangent

The angle whose tangent is a given number. Write \(\tan^{-1}(x)\). The \(-1\) is not an exponent.

Example: \(\tan^{-1}(1) = 45^\circ\)

See Also: tangent, inverse sine

isosceles trapezoid

A trapezoid whose legs are congruent. Each pair of base angles is congruent, and the diagonals are congruent.

isosceles triangle

A triangle with at least two sides the same length. The angles across from the equal sides are equal.

Example:

A triangle with sides \(5\), \(5\), and \(8\) is isosceles.

See Also: equilateral triangle

kite

A quadrilateral with two pairs of congruent sides that are next to each other. The diagonals are perpendicular.

See Also: rhombus

lateral area

The area of the sides of a solid, without the base or bases.

Example: The lateral area of a cylinder is \(2\pi r h\).

See Also: surface area

leg

One of the two sides of a right triangle that form the right angle.

See Also: hypotenuse

legs of an isosceles triangle

The two congruent sides of an isosceles triangle.

Example:

In \(\triangle ABC\) with \(AB \cong AC\), the legs are \(\overline{AB}\) and \(\overline{AC}\).

See Also: base of an isosceles triangle

like radicals

Radicals with the same radicand. You can add them: \(3\sqrt{2} + 5\sqrt{2} = 8\sqrt{2}\).

See Also: radical

line

A straight path of points that goes on forever in both directions. Write \(\overleftrightarrow{AB}\) or name it with one small letter, like line \(\ell\).

See Also: segment, ray

line of reflection

The line a figure is flipped over in a reflection. It is the perpendicular bisector of the segment from each point to its image.

Example:

The \(y\)-axis is the line of reflection when \((2, 5)\) goes to \((-2, 5)\).

See Also: perpendicular bisector

line of symmetry

A line that divides a figure into two halves that are mirror images of each other.

Example:

A square has 4 lines of symmetry.

See Also: line symmetry

line symmetry

A figure has line symmetry if a reflection over a line maps the figure onto itself.

Example:

The letter A has line symmetry. A vertical line cuts it into two matching halves.

See Also: line of symmetry

linear pair

Two adjacent angles whose outside sides are opposite rays. The angles in a linear pair are supplementary.

Example:

If \(m\angle 1 = 62^\circ\) and \(\angle 1\) and \(\angle 2\) are a linear pair, then \(m\angle 2 = 118^\circ\).

See Also: adjacent angles, supplementary

major arc

An arc that is more than half of a circle. Its measure is more than \(180^\circ\). We name it with three letters.

Example: If a minor arc measures \(70^\circ\), the major arc measures \(360^\circ - 70^\circ = 290^\circ\).

See Also: minor arc, semicircle

measure of an angle

How far an angle opens, in degrees. Write \(m\angle ABC = 40^\circ\). Read it as “the measure of angle \(ABC\) is 40 degrees.”

midpoint

The point exactly halfway between the endpoints of a segment. It cuts the segment into two congruent segments.

Example:

If \(M\) is the midpoint of \(\overline{AB}\) and \(AM = 6\), then \(MB = 6\) and \(AB = 12\).

See Also: segment bisector

midsegment of a trapezoid

The segment that connects the midpoints of the two legs of a trapezoid. It is parallel to the bases. Its length is the average of the base lengths.

Example:

If the bases are 12 and 28, the midsegment is \(\dfrac{12 + 28}{2} = 20\).

midsegment of a triangle

A segment that joins the midpoints of two sides of a triangle. It is parallel to the third side and half as long.

Example: If \(D\) and \(E\) are midpoints of \(\overline{AB}\) and \(\overline{AC}\) and \(BC = 18\), then \(DE = 9\).

See Also: midpoint, similar

minor arc

An arc that is less than half of a circle. Its measure is less than \(180^\circ\). We name it with its two endpoints.

Example: \(\overset{\frown}{AB}\) with \(m\overset{\frown}{AB} = 70^\circ\).

See Also: major arc, semicircle

net

A flat pattern that folds up into a solid. A net shows every face of the solid.

Example: A cube has a net made of \(6\) squares.

See Also: surface area

oblique

Slanted. In an oblique prism or cylinder, the sides lean over and are not at a right angle to the bases. The height is still measured straight up, at a right angle to the bases.

See Also: prism, cylinder

obtuse angle

An angle that measures more than \(90^\circ\) and less than \(180^\circ\).

See Also: acute angle, straight angle

opposite rays

Two rays with the same endpoint that go in opposite directions. Together they make a line.

opposite reciprocal

The number you get by flipping a fraction and changing its sign. Slopes of perpendicular lines are opposite reciprocals.

Example: The opposite reciprocal of \(\dfrac{2}{3}\) is \(-\dfrac{3}{2}\).

See Also: slope

opposite side

In a right triangle, the leg across from a chosen acute angle. It does not touch that angle.

Example: In \(\triangle ABC\) with the right angle at \(C\), side \(BC\) is opposite \(\angle A\).

See Also: adjacent side, sine

order of rotational symmetry

The number of times a figure fits onto itself in one full turn. The smallest angle of rotation is \(\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}\) when the order is \(n\).

Example:

A square has order 4, so its smallest angle of rotation is \(\dfrac{360^\circ}{4} = 90^\circ\).

See Also: rotational symmetry

orientation

The order, clockwise or counterclockwise, in which the labeled points of a figure go around. A reflection reverses orientation.

Example:

If \(A \rightarrow B \rightarrow C\) goes counterclockwise, then \(A' \rightarrow B' \rightarrow C'\) goes clockwise after a reflection.

See Also: reflection

parallel

Parallel lines are in the same plane and never meet. They stay the same distance apart. The symbol is \(\parallel\). In a figure, matching arrows mark parallel lines.

Example:

\(\ell \parallel m\) is read “line \(\ell\) is parallel to line \(m\).”

See Also: perpendicular, skew

parallelogram

A quadrilateral with both pairs of opposite sides parallel.

See Also: rectangle, rhombus

partition

To divide a segment into parts. A point partitions a segment in the ratio \(a:b\) when the two parts have lengths in the ratio \(a\) to \(b\).

Example: \((4, 4)\) partitions the segment from \((1, 2)\) to \((13, 10)\) in the ratio \(1:3\).

perfect square

A number that is the square of a whole number, such as 1, 4, 9, 16, 25.

Example: 49 is a perfect square because \(7^2 = 49\).

See Also: square root

perimeter

The distance around a polygon. It is the sum of the side lengths.

Example: A rectangle 6 by 4 has perimeter \(6 + 4 + 6 + 4 = 20\).

See Also: area

perpendicular

Perpendicular lines cross to make right angles. The symbol is \(\perp\).

Example:

\(p \perp q\) is read “line \(p\) is perpendicular to line \(q\).”

See Also: parallel, right angle

perpendicular bisector

A line that goes through the midpoint of a segment and is perpendicular to it.

Example:

The line \(x = 3\) is the perpendicular bisector of the segment from \((1, 4)\) to \((5, 4)\).

See Also: midpoint, perpendicular

pi

The number \(\pi\) is the circumference of a circle divided by its diameter. It is the same for every circle. \(\pi \approx 3.14159\).

Example: A circle with \(d = 10\) has \(C = 10\pi \approx 31.42\).

See Also: circumference

plane

A flat surface that goes on forever in every direction. Name it with one capital letter, like plane \(P\), or with three points in it that are not on one line.

point

An exact location. A point has no size. Name it with a capital letter, like point \(A\).

point of tangency

The one point where a tangent line touches a circle.

Example: In the figure, the tangent line touches the circle at \(T\), the point of tangency.

See Also: tangent to a circle

polygon

A closed figure made of three or more segments, called sides, that meet only at their endpoints. A polygon with \(n\) sides is an \(n\)-gon. The angles of a polygon with \(n\) sides add to \((n - 2) \cdot 180^\circ\).

Example:

Triangle (3 sides), quadrilateral (4), pentagon (5), hexagon (6), heptagon (7), octagon (8), nonagon (9), decagon (10).

See Also: regular polygon, convex polygon

polyhedron

A solid whose faces are all flat polygons. Prisms and pyramids are polyhedrons. Cylinders, cones, and spheres are not.

See Also: face, edge, vertex of a solid

population density

The number of people living in each unit of area, such as people per square mile: \(\dfrac{\text{population}}{\text{area}}\).

Example: A town of \(9{,}000\) people on \(6\) square miles has \(1{,}500\) people per square mile.

See Also: density, area

preimage

The original figure, before a transformation.

Example:

If \(\triangle ABC\) is slid to \(\triangle A'B'C'\), then \(\triangle ABC\) is the preimage.

See Also: image

prism

A polyhedron with two parallel, congruent faces called bases. The other faces are parallelograms. A prism is named by its base, such as a triangular prism.

Example: A cereal box is a rectangular prism.

See Also: pyramid, cylinder

proportion

An equation that says two ratios are equal.

Example: \(\dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{8}{12}\) is a proportion.

See Also: ratio, cross products

proportional

Two lists of numbers are proportional when the ratios of matching numbers are all equal.

Example: The sides 4, 6, 8 and 6, 9, 12 are proportional. Each ratio is 1.5.

See Also: proportion

protractor

A tool that measures angles in degrees. It has an outer scale and an inner scale. Read the scale that starts at \(0\) on one side of the angle.

pyramid

A polyhedron with one polygon base. The other faces are triangles that meet at one vertex, the apex.

Example: A square pyramid has 5 faces.

See Also: prism, cone

Pythagorean theorem

In a right triangle, the sum of the squares of the legs equals the square of the hypotenuse: \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\).

Example: Legs 3 and 4 give \(9 + 16 = 25\), so \(c = 5\).

See Also: hypotenuse, Pythagorean triple

Pythagorean triple

Three whole numbers \(a\), \(b\), \(c\) with \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\).

Example: 3, 4, 5 and 5, 12, 13.

See Also: Pythagorean theorem

quadrilateral

A polygon with four sides. The four angles add to \(360^\circ\).

See Also: parallelogram, trapezoid, kite

radian

A unit for measuring angles. One radian is the central angle whose arc is as long as the radius. A full turn is \(2\pi\) radians, so \(180^\circ = \pi\) radians. Radian measure \(\theta = \dfrac{s}{r}\).

Example: \(90^\circ = \dfrac{\pi}{2}\) radians.

See Also: degree, arc length, central angle

radical

An expression with a root sign, such as \(\sqrt{50}\) or \(3\sqrt{2}\). The number under the sign is the radicand.

See Also: radicand

radicand

The number under a radical sign. In \(\sqrt{50}\) the radicand is 50.

See Also: radical

radius

A segment from the center of a circle to a point on the circle. It is also the length of that segment. The plural is radii. All radii of a circle are equal.

Example: In circle \(P\), \(\overline{PA}\) is a radius.

See Also: diameter, center of a circle

ratio

A comparison of two numbers by division. It can be written \(a : b\), \(\dfrac{a}{b}\), or “a to b”.

Example: 3 red tiles and 5 blue tiles give the ratio \(3 : 5\) of red to blue.

See Also: proportion

rationalize

To rewrite a fraction so that the denominator has no radical.

Example: \(\dfrac{6}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{6\sqrt{2}}{2} = 3\sqrt{2}\).

See Also: radical

ray

Part of a line that starts at one endpoint and goes on forever in one direction. Write \(\overrightarrow{AB}\): it starts at \(A\) and goes through \(B\).

See Also: opposite rays

rectangle

A parallelogram with a right angle.

See Also: parallelogram, square

reduction

A dilation with a scale factor between 0 and 1. The image is smaller than the original.

Example:

A scale factor of \(\dfrac{1}{3}\) makes a reduction.

See Also: enlargement

reflection

A transformation that flips a figure over a line. Each point and its image are the same distance from the line, on opposite sides.

Example:

Reflecting \((3, 4)\) over the \(x\)-axis gives \((3, -4)\).

See Also: line of reflection, orientation

regular polygon

A polygon with all sides the same length and all angles the same measure.

Example:

A stop sign is a regular octagon. Each angle is \(135^\circ\).

See Also: polygon

rhombus

A quadrilateral with four sides of equal length.

See Also: parallelogram, square

right angle

An angle that measures exactly \(90^\circ\). A small square at the vertex marks a right angle.

rigid motion

A transformation that keeps all lengths and all angle measures the same. Translations, reflections, and rotations are rigid motions.

Example:

A dilation with scale factor 2 is not a rigid motion, because it doubles the lengths.

See Also: translation

rotation

A transformation that turns every point of a figure by the same angle about a fixed point, the center of rotation.

Example:

Rotating \((2, 1)\) by \(90^\circ\) counterclockwise about the origin gives \((-1, 2)\).

See Also: center of rotation, angle of rotation

rotational symmetry

A figure has rotational symmetry if a rotation of less than \(360^\circ\) about its center maps the figure onto itself.

Example:

A regular pentagon fits onto itself after a turn of \(72^\circ\).

See Also: order of rotational symmetry

same-side interior angles

Two angles between the two lines, on the same side of the transversal. If the two lines are parallel, same-side interior angles are supplementary.

See Also: supplementary, alternate interior angles

SAS

Side, angle, side. If two sides and the included angle of one triangle are congruent to two sides and the included angle of another triangle, the triangles are congruent.

See Also: included angle

SAS similarity

If two pairs of matching sides are proportional and the included angles are equal, the triangles are similar.

Example: Sides 6 and 9 with a \(40^\circ\) angle between them, and sides 10 and 15 with a \(40^\circ\) angle between them.

See Also: SSS similarity, included angle

scale

The ratio that compares a length on a drawing or map to the real length.

Example: A scale of \(1 \text{ in} : 20 \text{ ft}\) means 1 inch on the drawing is 20 feet in real life.

See Also: scale drawing, scale factor

scale drawing

A drawing that has the same shape as the real object but a different size. Maps and floor plans are scale drawings.

Example: A floor plan drawn with the scale \(1 \text{ in} : 4 \text{ ft}\).

See Also: scale

scale factor

The number \(k\) that a dilation multiplies every length by. It equals \(\dfrac{\text{image length}}{\text{original length}}\).

Example:

A side of 4 becomes 10, so the scale factor is \(\dfrac{10}{4} = 2.5\).

See Also: enlargement, reduction

scalene triangle

A triangle with no two sides the same length.

See Also: isosceles triangle, equilateral triangle

sector

The region inside a circle between two radii and the arc they cut off. Area \(= \dfrac{\text{central angle}}{360^\circ} \cdot \pi r^2\).

Example: A slice of pizza is a sector.

See Also: central angle, arc length

segment

Part of a line between two endpoints. Write \(\overline{AB}\). Its length is written \(AB\), with no bar.

segment bisector

A point, line, ray, or segment that goes through the midpoint of a segment.

See Also: midpoint, angle bisector

semicircle

Half of a circle. It is an arc that measures \(180^\circ\). The endpoints of a semicircle are the ends of a diameter.

Example: \(\overset{\frown}{BAC}\), where \(\overline{BC}\) is a diameter.

See Also: arc, diameter

sequence of transformations

Two or more transformations done one after another. The first image is \(A'\) and the second image is \(A''\).

Example:

Translate a figure 3 units right, then reflect it over the \(y\)-axis.

See Also: transformation

shaded region

A part of a figure that is colored in. To find its area, add or subtract the areas of simple shapes.

Example: A square with a circle cut out: area of the square minus area of the circle.

See Also: area, annulus

similar

Figures that have the same shape but not always the same size. Matching angles are equal and matching sides are proportional. The symbol is \(\sim\).

Example: A 4 by 6 photo and an 8 by 12 photo are similar.

See Also: similarity statement, scale factor

similarity statement

A statement such as \(\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF\). The order of the letters shows which vertices match.

Example: \(\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF\) means \(\angle A\) matches \(\angle D\) and \(\overline{BC}\) matches \(\overline{EF}\).

See Also: similar, corresponding parts

sine

For an acute angle \(A\) in a right triangle, \(\sin A = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\).

Example: \(\sin A = \dfrac{8}{17}\) when the opposite side is \(8\) and the hypotenuse is \(17\).

See Also: cosine, tangent

skew

Skew lines are not in the same plane. They do not meet and they are not parallel.

Example:

The bottom front edge of a box and the vertical edge at its back corner are skew.

See Also: parallel

slant height

For a pyramid, the height of a triangle face, measured along the face from the base edge to the apex. For a cone, the distance along the surface from the edge of the base to the apex. It is written \(\ell\).

Example: A cone with radius \(3\) and height \(4\) has slant height \(5\).

See Also: pyramid, cone, Pythagorean theorem

slope

A number that tells how steep a line is: the change in \(y\) divided by the change in \(x\), or rise over run.

Example: The line through \((1, 2)\) and \((4, 8)\) has slope \(\dfrac{8 - 2}{4 - 1} = 2\).

See Also: opposite reciprocal

solid

A three-dimensional shape. It takes up space.

Example: A box, a can, a ball, and a tent are solids.

See Also: polyhedron

solid of revolution

A solid made by spinning a flat shape around a line.

Example: Spinning a rectangle around one of its sides makes a cylinder.

See Also: axis of revolution

special right triangle

A right triangle whose sides follow a pattern you can use without a calculator. The two special right triangles are the \(45^\circ\)-\(45^\circ\)-\(90^\circ\) triangle and the \(30^\circ\)-\(60^\circ\)-\(90^\circ\) triangle.

See Also: 45-45-90 triangle, 30-60-90 triangle

sphere

The set of all points in space that are the same distance (the radius) from one point, the center. A ball is a sphere.

See Also: radius, circle

square

A quadrilateral that is both a rectangle and a rhombus: four equal sides and four right angles.

See Also: rectangle, rhombus

square root

A number that gives the original number when you multiply it by itself. In geometry we use the positive root.

Example: \(\sqrt{49} = 7\).

See Also: perfect square, radical

SSS

Side, side, side. If three sides of one triangle are congruent to three sides of another triangle, the triangles are congruent.

See Also: SAS

SSS similarity

If the three pairs of matching sides of two triangles are proportional, the triangles are similar.

Example: Sides 4, 6, 8 and 10, 15, 20 have equal ratios of 2.5, so the triangles are similar.

See Also: AA similarity, SAS similarity

straight angle

An angle that measures exactly \(180^\circ\). Its sides are opposite rays, so it looks like a line.

straightedge

A tool for drawing straight lines. A straightedge has no number marks.

supplementary

Two angles whose measures add to \(180^\circ\). Each angle is the supplement of the other.

Example:

\(110^\circ\) and \(70^\circ\) are supplementary because \(110 + 70 = 180\).

See Also: complementary, linear pair

surface area

The total area of all the faces (or the whole outside surface) of a solid. It is measured in square units.

Example: A box \(8\) in by \(3\) in by \(5\) in has surface area \(158\) square inches.

See Also: lateral area, net, area

tangent

For an acute angle \(A\) in a right triangle, \(\tan A = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}\). It is the slope of a line that makes angle \(A\) with the horizontal.

Example: \(\tan A = \dfrac{8}{15}\) when the opposite side is \(8\) and the adjacent side is \(15\).

See Also: sine, cosine, slope

tangent to a circle

A line in the plane of a circle that touches the circle at exactly one point. It is perpendicular to the radius at that point. Two tangent segments from one outside point are equal.

Example: A line tangent at \(T(-3, 4)\) to the circle \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\) has slope \(\dfrac{3}{4}\).

See Also: point of tangency, radius, perpendicular

transformation

A rule that moves or changes every point of a figure. Translations, reflections, rotations, and dilations are transformations.

Example:

The rule \((x, y) \rightarrow (x + 3, y - 2)\) is a transformation.

See Also: preimage, image

translation

A transformation that slides every point of a figure the same distance in the same direction. It does not turn or flip the figure.

Example:

\((x, y) \rightarrow (x + 5, y - 1)\) moves a figure 5 units right and 1 unit down.

See Also: rigid motion

transversal

A line that crosses two or more other lines at different points.

See Also: corresponding angles

trapezoid

A quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel sides. The parallel sides are the bases. The other two sides are the legs.

See Also: isosceles trapezoid, midsegment of a trapezoid

triangle

A polygon with three sides and three angles. The angles of a triangle add to \(180^\circ\). Write \(\triangle ABC\).

triangle inequality

The two shorter sides of a triangle always add to more than the longest side.

Example: 3, 4, 9 cannot make a triangle because \(3 + 4 < 9\).

trigonometric ratio

A ratio of two sides of a right triangle. The three trigonometric ratios are sine, cosine, and tangent. Each depends only on the acute angle.

See Also: sine, cosine, tangent

vertex

The point where the two sides of an angle meet. The plural is vertices.

vertex angle

The angle of an isosceles triangle between the two congruent sides.

Example:

In \(\triangle ABC\) with \(AB \cong AC\), the vertex angle is \(\angle A\).

See Also: base angles

vertex of a solid

A point where three or more edges of a polyhedron meet. A cube has 8 vertices. The plural is vertices.

See Also: edge, vertex

vertical angles

The two angles across from each other when two lines cross. Vertical angles are equal.

Example:

If two lines cross and one angle is \(60^\circ\), the angle across from it is also \(60^\circ\).

See Also: linear pair

volume

The amount of space inside a solid. It is measured in cubic units.

Example: A box \(6\) ft by \(4\) ft by \(3\) ft has volume \(72\) cubic feet.

See Also: cubic unit, surface area
