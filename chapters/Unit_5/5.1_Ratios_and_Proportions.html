5.1 - Ratios and Proportions

Amira is making lemonade for a school party. Her recipe uses 2 cups of lemon juice and 3 cups of water. She needs more, so she doubles the recipe. Now she uses 4 cups of juice and 6 cups of water.

The amounts changed. The taste did not, because the ratio of juice to water is still the same. Maps, floor plans, and recipes all work this way. In this lesson you learn to write ratios and to solve for a missing number. Later in this unit, these skills help you find missing lengths in figures with the same shape.

ratio, proportion, cross products, scale drawing, scale

5.1.1 - Ratios

A ratio compares two numbers by division. Amira’s recipe has 2 cups of juice for every 3 cups of water. The ratio of juice to water is 2 to 3.

You can write the same ratio three ways:

  • 2 to 3
  • \(2 : 3\)
  • \(\dfrac{2}{3}\)

A ratio can be simplified like a fraction. Divide both numbers by the same number. The ratio \(4 : 6\) simplifies to \(2 : 3\) because \(4 \div 2 = 2\) and \(6 \div 2 = 3\).

The ratio of juice to water is \(2 : 3\). The ratio of water to juice is \(3 : 2\). These are different. The first number goes with the first thing named.

Example 1: Write and simplify a ratio

A box has 15 red tiles and 25 blue tiles.

  1. Write the ratio of red tiles to blue tiles.

  2. Write the ratio of blue tiles to all the tiles.

  3. \(15 : 25\). Divide both by 5. The ratio is \(3 : 5\).

  4. There are \(15 + 25 = 40\) tiles. The ratio is \(25 : 40\). Divide both by 5. The ratio is \(5 : 8\).

  1. Write the ratio of tablets to laptops in lowest terms.

  2. Write the ratio of laptops to all the devices in lowest terms.

  1. \(30 : 18 = 5 : 3\)

  2. \(18 : 48 = 3 : 8\)

Use the same units. To compare 2 feet and 8 inches, change them to the same unit first.

Example 3: Ratios with units

Ahmad has a board that is 2 feet long. He cuts a piece that is 8 inches long. Write the ratio of the board to the piece in lowest terms.

Change feet to inches: \(2 \text{ ft} = 24 \text{ in}\).

The ratio is \(24 : 8\). Divide both by 8. The ratio is \(3 : 1\).

The board is 3 times as long as the piece.

What is the ratio of 3 yards to 6 feet?

\(3 \text{ yd} = 9 \text{ ft}\). The ratio is \(9 : 6 = 3 : 2\).

5.1.2 - Proportions

A proportion is an equation that says two ratios are equal. Start with Amira’s lemonade. The recipe uses 2 cups of juice for 3 cups of water. How much juice goes with 12 cups of water?

Let \(x\) be the cups of juice. Juice is on top and water is on the bottom in both ratios:

\[ \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{x}{12} \]

Multiply both sides by \(3\) and by \(12\). The left side becomes \(2 \cdot 12\). The right side becomes \(3 \cdot x\). So \(24 = 3x\). Then \(x = 8\).

That shortcut always works. It is called cross products. Multiply along the two diagonals.

\[ \dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{c}{d} \quad \text{means} \quad ad = bc \]

Example 5: Solve a proportion

Solve \(\dfrac{x}{12} = \dfrac{5}{8}\).

Cross products: \(8 \cdot x = 12 \cdot 5\).

\(8x = 60\)

\(x = 7.5\)

Check: \(\dfrac{7.5}{12} = 0.625\) and \(\dfrac{5}{8} = 0.625\).

\(\dfrac{x}{10} = \dfrac{7}{4}\)

\(4x = 70\), so \(x = 17.5\).

Example 7: A proportion from a word problem

Moses walks 6 miles in 2 hours. He keeps the same speed. How long does it take him to walk 15 miles?

Put miles on top and hours on the bottom in both ratios. Let \(t\) be the hours.

\(\dfrac{6}{2} = \dfrac{15}{t}\)

\(6t = 2 \cdot 15 = 30\)

\(t = 5\)

It takes 5 hours.

A printer prints 45 pages in 6 minutes. How many pages does it print in 20 minutes?

\(\dfrac{45}{6} = \dfrac{p}{20}\), so \(6p = 900\) and \(p = 150\) pages.

Sometimes the unknown is part of an expression. Use cross products, then solve the equation.

Example 9: Expressions in a proportion

Solve \(\dfrac{5}{x + 2} = \dfrac{4}{x}\).

Cross products: \(5 \cdot x = 4(x + 2)\).

\(5x = 4x + 8\)

\(x = 8\)

Check: \(\dfrac{5}{10} = 0.5\) and \(\dfrac{4}{8} = 0.5\).

\(\dfrac{7}{x} = \dfrac{3}{x - 4}\)

\(7(x - 4) = 3x\), so \(7x - 28 = 3x\) and \(4x = 28\). \(x = 7\).

Example 11: A proportion with two answers

Solve \(\dfrac{x - 2}{5} = \dfrac{20}{x - 2}\).

Cross products: \((x - 2)(x - 2) = 5 \cdot 20\).

\((x - 2)^2 = 100\)

\(x - 2 = 10\) or \(x - 2 = -10\)

\(x = 12\) or \(x = -8\)

Both work in the equation. In a figure, a length cannot be negative, so you would keep only \(x = 12\).

\(\dfrac{x + 1}{4} = \dfrac{9}{x + 1}\)

\((x + 1)^2 = 36\), so \(x + 1 = 6\) or \(x + 1 = -6\). \(x = 5\) or \(x = -7\).

5.1.3 - Scale Drawings and Maps

A scale drawing is a smaller or larger copy of something real. A map and a floor plan are scale drawings. The scale tells how a length on the drawing matches the real length.

A scale of \(1 \text{ in} : 20 \text{ ft}\) means that 1 inch on the drawing stands for 20 feet in real life. Put the drawing length in one ratio and the real length in the other. Keep the same units in the same place.

Example 13: Use a scale

The plan of a lot has the scale \(1 \text{ in} : 20 \text{ ft}\). On the plan, the lot is 3.5 in long and 2 in wide.

  1. Find the real length of the lot.

  2. A shed is 12 ft wide. How wide is the shed on the plan?

  3. Let \(L\) be the real length in feet.

\(\dfrac{1}{20} = \dfrac{3.5}{L}\), so \(L = 20 \cdot 3.5 = 70\) ft.

The lot is 70 ft long (and \(2 \cdot 20 = 40\) ft wide).

  1. Let \(d\) be the width on the plan in inches.

\(\dfrac{1}{20} = \dfrac{d}{12}\), so \(20d = 12\) and \(d = 0.6\).

The shed is 0.6 in wide on the plan.

  1. Two towns are 3.5 in apart on the map. Find the real distance.

  2. Two lakes are 50 mi apart. How far apart are they on the map?

  1. \(3.5 \cdot 8 = 28\) mi.

  2. \(\dfrac{1}{8} = \dfrac{d}{50}\), so \(8d = 50\) and \(d = 6.25\) in.

A scale can also have no units, like \(1 : 50\). Then both numbers use the same unit. On a \(1 : 50\) plan, 1 cm on the plan stands for 50 cm in real life.

5.1.4 - Ratios in a Figure

A ratio can split a total into parts. Write each part as a number times \(x\). Add the parts to equal the total. Solve for \(x\).

Example 15: Angles in a ratio

The angles of a triangle are in the ratio \(2 : 3 : 4\). Find each angle.

The angles add to \(180^\circ\).

\(2x + 3x + 4x = 180\)

\(9x = 180\), so \(x = 20\).

The angles are \(2(20) = 40^\circ\), \(3(20) = 60^\circ\), and \(4(20) = 80^\circ\).

Check: \(40 + 60 + 80 = 180\).

Find each angle. Then say whether the triangle is acute, right, or obtuse.

\(x + 2x + 6x = 180\), so \(9x = 180\) and \(x = 20\).

The angles are \(20^\circ\), \(40^\circ\), and \(120^\circ\). The triangle is obtuse.

Example 17: A segment in a ratio

Point \(P\) is on \(\overline{AB}\). \(AB = 40\) in. The ratio \(AP : PB\) is \(3 : 5\). Find \(AP\) and \(PB\).

\(AP + PB = AB\)

\(3x + 5x = 40\)

\(8x = 40\), so \(x = 5\).

\(AP = 3(5) = 15\) in and \(PB = 5(5) = 25\) in.

A mix is cement : sand : gravel \(= 1 : 2 : 3\) by volume. The total is 27 cubic feet. How much of each is there?

\(x + 2x + 3x = 27\), so \(6x = 27\) and \(x = 4.5\).

Cement: 4.5 cubic ft. Sand: 9 cubic ft. Gravel: 13.5 cubic ft.
