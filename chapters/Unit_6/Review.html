6.1 - Special Right Triangles

Example 1: A 30-60-90 triangle has a long leg of 9. Find the short leg and the hypotenuse. Give exact answers.

Long leg \(= s\sqrt{3} = 9\), so \(s = \dfrac{9}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{9\sqrt{3}}{3} = 3\sqrt{3}\).

Hypotenuse \(= 2s = 6\sqrt{3} \approx 10.4\).

6.2 - The Trigonometric Ratios

Example 2: A right triangle has legs 8 and 15 and hypotenuse 17. Find \(\sin B\), \(\cos B\), and \(\tan B\) for the angle \(B\) across from the leg 15. Round to the nearest thousandth.

Opposite \(= 15\), adjacent \(= 8\), hypotenuse \(= 17\).

\(\sin B = \dfrac{15}{17} \approx 0.882\), \(\cos B = \dfrac{8}{17} \approx 0.471\), \(\tan B = \dfrac{15}{8} = 1.875\).

6.3 - Solving for a Side

Example 3: A ladder leans on a wall. It makes a \(68^\circ\) angle with the ground and its base is 4 feet from the wall. How long is the ladder? Round to the nearest tenth.

The base is adjacent to \(68^\circ\). The ladder is the hypotenuse. Use cosine.

\(\cos 68^\circ = \dfrac{4}{L}\), so \(L = \dfrac{4}{\cos 68^\circ} \approx 10.7\) ft.

6.4 - Solving for an Angle

Example 4: A ramp rises 22 inches over a run of 240 inches. Find the angle of the ramp. Round to the nearest tenth of a degree.

Rise is opposite and run is adjacent. Use tangent.

\(\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{22}{240}\right) \approx 5.2^\circ\).

6.5 - Sine and Cosine of Complementary Angles

Example 5: Write \(\cos 34^\circ\) as the sine of an angle. Then find \(x\) if \(\sin(x + 4^\circ) = \cos(2x - 1^\circ)\).

\(\cos 34^\circ = \sin(90^\circ - 34^\circ) = \sin 56^\circ\).

The angles add to \(90^\circ\): \((x + 4) + (2x - 1) = 90\), so \(3x = 87\) and \(x = 29\).

6.6 - Angles of Elevation and Depression

Example 6: From the top of a 120-foot lighthouse, the angle of depression to a boat is \(14^\circ\). How far is the boat from the base? Round to the nearest foot.

The angle at the boat is also \(14^\circ\) (alternate interior angles). The height is opposite and the distance is adjacent.

\(\tan 14^\circ = \dfrac{120}{d}\), so \(d = \dfrac{120}{\tan 14^\circ} \approx 481\) ft.

6.7 - Modeling with Right Triangles

Example 7: A house is 32 feet wide. The roof has a \(5/12\) pitch and no overhang. Find the roof angle and the rafter length. Round to the nearest tenth.

The run is \(16\) ft and the rise is \(16 \cdot \dfrac{5}{12} \approx 6.7\) ft.

Angle: \(\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{5}{12}\right) \approx 22.6^\circ\). Rafter: \(\sqrt{16^2 + 6.7^2} \approx 17.3\) ft.

✍️ Practice On Your Own

6.1 - I can use the 45-45-90 and 30-60-90 triangle patterns.

  1. A 45-45-90 triangle has a leg of 9 cm. Find the hypotenuse. Give the exact answer and the answer to the nearest tenth.

  2. A 45-45-90 triangle has a hypotenuse of 18 in. Find each leg. Rationalize and round to the nearest tenth.

  3. A 30-60-90 triangle has a short leg of 6 m. Find the long leg and the hypotenuse. Round to the nearest tenth.

  4. A 30-60-90 triangle has a hypotenuse of 20 ft. Find both legs. Round to the nearest tenth.

  5. A square tile has a diagonal of 30 cm. Find its side. Round to the nearest tenth.

  6. Ana says that in a 30-60-90 triangle with a long leg of 9, the hypotenuse is 18. Find her mistake and the correct hypotenuse (nearest tenth).

6.2 - I can name the sides and write the sine, cosine, and tangent.

  1. A right triangle has legs 9 and 40 and hypotenuse 41. For the angle \(B\) across from the 40, write \(\sin B\), \(\cos B\), and \(\tan B\) as fractions.

  2. Use a calculator. Find \(\sin 52^\circ\), \(\cos 52^\circ\), and \(\tan 52^\circ\) to the nearest thousandth.

  3. One right triangle has a \(30^\circ\) angle, a hypotenuse of 10, and an opposite side of 5. Another has a hypotenuse of 24 and an opposite side of 12. Show that \(\sin 30^\circ\) is the same in both.

  4. A line through the origin makes a \(35^\circ\) angle with the \(x\)-axis. Find its slope to the nearest thousandth.

  5. In a right triangle, \(\tan A = \dfrac{3}{4}\). Find the hypotenuse if the legs are 3 and 4, then find \(\sin A\) and \(\cos A\).

  6. Ben says \(\sin A = \dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\). Find his mistake.

6.3 - I can find a side of a right triangle with sine, cosine, or tangent.

  1. An angle is \(38^\circ\) and the hypotenuse is 25. Find the opposite side. Round to the nearest tenth.

  2. An angle is \(55^\circ\) and the adjacent side is 14. Find the hypotenuse. Round to the nearest tenth.

  3. An angle is \(27^\circ\) and the adjacent side is 60. Find the opposite side. Round to the nearest tenth.

  4. A kite string is 80 feet long and makes a \(62^\circ\) angle with the ground. How high is the kite? Ignore sag. Round to the nearest tenth.

  5. A 5-meter ladder makes a \(72^\circ\) angle with the ground. How far is its base from the wall? Round to the nearest tenth.

  6. Dara solves \(\tan 40^\circ = \dfrac{9}{x}\) and writes \(x = 9\tan 40^\circ \approx 7.6\). Find her mistake and the correct \(x\) (nearest tenth).

6.4 - I can find an angle of a right triangle with an inverse ratio.

  1. Find \(A\) if \(\sin A = 0.6\). Round to the nearest tenth of a degree.

  2. A right triangle has legs 9 and 14. Find the angle across from the 9. Round to the nearest tenth of a degree.

  3. The hypotenuse is 30 and the adjacent side is 24. Find both acute angles. Round to the nearest tenth of a degree.

  4. A ramp rises 20 inches over a run of 200 inches. Find the angle of the ramp. Round to the nearest tenth of a degree.

  5. A stair has a riser of 7 inches and a tread of 11 inches. Find the angle of the stairs. Round to the nearest tenth of a degree.

  6. \(A(1, 2)\) and \(B(9, 7)\) are on a line. Find the angle the line makes with the \(x\)-axis. Round to the nearest tenth of a degree.

6.5 - I can use sine and cosine of complementary angles.

  1. Write \(\cos 20^\circ\) as the sine of an angle.

  2. \(\sin 28^\circ \approx 0.469\). What is \(\cos 62^\circ\) to the nearest thousandth?

  3. \(\tan 18^\circ \approx 0.325\). Use this to find \(\tan 72^\circ\) to the nearest hundredth.

  4. Solve \(\sin x = \cos(2x + 9^\circ)\).

  5. In a right triangle, \(\sin A = \dfrac{7}{25}\). What is \(\cos B\) for the other acute angle \(B\)?

  6. Lia says \(\sin 40^\circ = \cos 40^\circ\). Give a counterexample with numbers.

6.6 - I can solve problems with angles of elevation and depression.

  1. You stand 60 m from a building. Your eyes are 1.6 m above the ground. The angle of elevation to the top is \(48^\circ\). How tall is the building? Round to the nearest tenth.

  2. From the top of an 85-foot cliff, the angle of depression to a boat is \(33^\circ\). How far is the boat from the base of the cliff? Round to the nearest tenth.

  3. A plane is 2000 feet above the ground. The angle of depression to the end of the runway is \(4^\circ\). Find the ground distance to the nearest foot.

  4. You stand 120 feet from a building. The angle of elevation to the bottom of a flagpole on the roof is \(25^\circ\). To the top it is \(31^\circ\). How tall is the flagpole? Ignore your eye height. Round to the nearest tenth.

  5. A student says the angle of depression from a tower to a car is \(55^\circ\), so the angle of elevation from the car to the tower is \(35^\circ\). Find the mistake.

6.7 - I can solve real problems with right triangles.

  1. A porch is 21 inches high. A ramp follows the 1-to-12 rule. Find the run in feet and the ramp length in feet. Round to the nearest tenth.

  2. A house is 36 feet wide. The roof has a \(5/12\) pitch and no overhang. Find the roof angle and the rafter length. Round to the nearest tenth.

  3. A boat sails 15 km on a bearing of \(040^\circ\). How far north and how far east of its start is it? Round to the nearest tenth.

  4. A drone hovers 50 m above the ground between two markers. The angles of depression to the markers are \(35^\circ\) and \(55^\circ\). How far apart are the markers? Round to the nearest tenth.

  5. A 12-foot ladder is set so that its base is 1 foot from the wall for every 4 feet of height. How high does it reach? How far is its base from the wall? Round to the nearest tenth.
