6.5 - Sine and Cosine of Complementary Angles

A \(20\)-foot ladder leans against a wall. The ladder makes a \(70^\circ\) angle with the ground. It also makes a \(20^\circ\) angle with the wall.

Mia finds the height with \(20\sin 70^\circ\). Jamal finds it with \(20\cos 20^\circ\). They get the same answer. Is that luck? In this lesson you learn why the sine of one angle equals the cosine of the other angle.

complementary, sine, cosine, tangent, opposite side, adjacent side

6.5.1 - The Acute Angles Are Complementary

The three angles of a triangle add to \(180^\circ\). A right triangle has one \(90^\circ\) angle. So the other two angles add to \(90^\circ\):

\[ m\angle A + m\angle B = 90^\circ \qquad\text{so}\qquad m\angle B = 90^\circ - m\angle A. \]

Angles that add to \(90^\circ\) are complementary (Lesson 1.3). In a right triangle, the two acute angles are always complementary.

6.5.2 - Sine and Cosine Trade Places

Look at the triangle. The side \(a\) is opposite \(A\). It is adjacent to \(B\). The side \(b\) is adjacent to \(A\). It is opposite \(B\).

\[ \sin A = \frac{a}{c} \qquad \cos B = \frac{a}{c} \]

The two ratios are the same fraction. So \(\sin A = \cos B\). Since \(B = 90^\circ - A\):

\[ \sin A = \cos(90^\circ - A) \qquad\qquad \cos A = \sin(90^\circ - A) \]

The sine of an angle equals the cosine of its complement.

Check it with a calculator. Round to four decimal places.

Angle \(A\) \(\sin A\) Complement \(\cos(90^\circ - A)\)
\(15^\circ\) \(0.2588\) \(75^\circ\) \(0.2588\)
\(28^\circ\) \(0.4695\) \(62^\circ\) \(0.4695\)
\(40^\circ\) \(0.6428\) \(50^\circ\) \(0.6428\)
\(62^\circ\) \(0.8829\) \(28^\circ\) \(0.8829\)

In every row the two values match. That is the reason for the name cosine: it is the sine of the complement.

6.5.3 - Using the Rule

Example 1: Find a complement

Write each value as the sine or cosine of another angle.

  1. \(\cos 15^\circ = \sin(90^\circ - 15^\circ) = \sin 75^\circ\)

  2. \(\sin 41^\circ = \cos(90^\circ - 41^\circ) = \cos 49^\circ\)

Example 2: Use a known value

You know that \(\sin 28^\circ \approx 0.4695\). Find \(\cos 62^\circ\) without a calculator.

The angles \(28^\circ\) and \(62^\circ\) add to \(90^\circ\). So \(\cos 62^\circ = \sin 28^\circ \approx 0.4695\).

  1. Write \(\sin 12^\circ\) as a cosine.

  2. You know \(\cos 73^\circ \approx 0.2924\). Find \(\sin 17^\circ\).

  1. \(\cos 78^\circ\)

  2. \(\sin 17^\circ = \cos 73^\circ \approx 0.2924\)

6.5.4 - Solving Equations

If \(\sin(\text{one angle}) = \cos(\text{another angle})\), the two angles are complementary. They add to \(90^\circ\).

Example 3: Solve for \(x\)

Solve \(\sin(2x + 10)^\circ = \cos(3x)^\circ\).

The angles are complementary:

\[ (2x + 10) + 3x = 90 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 5x + 10 = 90 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 5x = 80 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 16. \]

Check: \(2(16) + 10 = 42\) and \(3(16) = 48\). Then \(\sin 42^\circ \approx 0.6691\) and \(\cos 48^\circ \approx 0.6691\). They match.

Example 4: Solve for \(x\)

Solve \(\sin(x + 20)^\circ = \cos(2x - 5)^\circ\).

\[ (x + 20) + (2x - 5) = 90 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 3x + 15 = 90 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 25. \]

The angles are \(45^\circ\) and \(45^\circ\). Check: \(\sin 45^\circ = \cos 45^\circ \approx 0.7071\).

  1. \(\sin(4x - 6)^\circ = \cos(x + 16)^\circ\)

  2. \(\sin(x + 5)^\circ = \cos(3x + 15)^\circ\)

  1. \((4x - 6) + (x + 16) = 90\), so \(5x + 10 = 90\) and \(x = 16\). The angles are \(58^\circ\) and \(32^\circ\).

  2. \((x + 5) + (3x + 15) = 90\), so \(4x + 20 = 90\) and \(x = 17.5\). The angles are \(22.5^\circ\) and \(67.5^\circ\).

\(\sin A = \cos A\) is true only when \(A = 45^\circ\). For other angles, \(\sin A\) and \(\cos A\) are different. For example, \(\sin 35^\circ \approx 0.5736\) but \(\cos 35^\circ \approx 0.8192\). The rule pairs an angle with its complement.

6.5.5 - Two More Facts

Tangent from sine and cosine. Divide the side ratios:

\[ \frac{\sin A}{\cos A} = \frac{a/c}{b/c} = \frac{a}{b} = \tan A. \]

Tangent and the complement. The side \(a\) is opposite \(A\) and adjacent to \(B\). So \(\tan A = \dfrac{a}{b}\) and \(\tan B = \dfrac{b}{a}\). These are reciprocals:

\[ \tan A \cdot \tan(90^\circ - A) = 1. \]

Example 5: Tangent from sine and cosine

Check that \(\tan 35^\circ = \dfrac{\sin 35^\circ}{\cos 35^\circ}\).

\(\dfrac{0.5736}{0.8192} \approx 0.7002\), and a calculator gives \(\tan 35^\circ \approx 0.7002\).

Example 6: Use the product rule

\(\tan 40^\circ \approx 0.8391\). Find \(\tan 50^\circ\) without a calculator.

The angles \(40^\circ\) and \(50^\circ\) are complementary, so \(\tan 50^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\tan 40^\circ} \approx \dfrac{1}{0.8391} \approx 1.1918\).

  1. Find \(\tan A\).

  2. Find \(\tan B\), where \(B\) is the other acute angle.

  1. \(\tan A = \dfrac{0.6}{0.8} = 0.75\)

  2. \(\tan B = \dfrac{1}{0.75} \approx 1.3333\). (Also \(\sin B = \cos A = 0.8\) and \(\cos B = \sin A = 0.6\), so \(\tan B = \frac{0.8}{0.6}\).)

Example 7: Two ways to find the height

A \(20\)-foot ladder makes a \(70^\circ\) angle with the ground and a \(20^\circ\) angle with the wall. Find how high it reaches in two ways. Round to the nearest tenth.

  • From the ground angle: \(h = 20\sin 70^\circ \approx 18.8\) feet.
  • From the wall angle: \(h\) is adjacent to \(20^\circ\), so \(h = 20\cos 20^\circ \approx 18.8\) feet.

Both ways give the same height, because \(\sin 70^\circ = \cos 20^\circ\).

Lucía says: “\(\sin 30^\circ = \cos 30^\circ\), because the sine of an angle is the cosine of the angle.” What is wrong?

The rule pairs an angle with its complement, not with itself. \(\sin 30^\circ = \cos 60^\circ = 0.5\). But \(\cos 30^\circ \approx 0.8660\).
