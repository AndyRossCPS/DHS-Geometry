6.6 - Angles of Elevation and Depression

A pilot lands a plane. A lifeguard watches a swimmer from a tall chair. A student looks up at the top of a cell tower. Each person looks along a line of sight. The angle the line of sight makes with the flat ground tells how high or how far away something is.

In this lesson you use that angle, and one side, to find a height or a distance you cannot measure with a tape.

angle of elevation, angle of depression, alternate interior angles, tangent

👥 Learn Together

6.6.1 - Elevation and Depression

Imagine a flat, level line from your eyes. This is the horizontal.

  • The angle of elevation is the angle from the horizontal up to your line of sight.
  • The angle of depression is the angle from the horizontal down to your line of sight.

Both angles are measured from the horizontal, never from the vertical.

A common error is to use the angle from the vertical (straight up). In the picture, the angle of elevation is \(62^\circ\). The angle from the vertical is \(28^\circ\). They add to \(90^\circ\). Use the one that starts at the horizontal.

6.6.2 - Looking Up and Looking Down

A person at \(A\) looks up at \(B\). A person at \(B\) looks down at \(A\). The two horizontal lines are parallel.

The line of sight is a transversal. The angle of elevation at \(A\) and the angle of depression at \(B\) are alternate interior angles (Lesson 1.4). Parallel lines make them equal.

The angle of elevation from \(A\) to \(B\) equals the angle of depression from \(B\) to \(A\).

6.6.3 - Finding Heights and Distances

Use these steps.

  1. Draw a picture. Draw the horizontal line and the line of sight.
  2. Mark the angle from the horizontal.
  3. Draw the right triangle. Label the sides opposite and adjacent to the angle.
  4. Pick sine, cosine, or tangent. Solve.
  5. If the observer’s eyes are above the ground, add the eye height to the height you find.

Example 1: A cell tower, with eye height

Hana stands \(80\) feet from a cell tower. Her eyes are \(5.5\) feet above the ground. The angle of elevation to the top is \(62^\circ\). How tall is the tower? Round to the nearest tenth.

The side \(x\) is the part of the tower above her eye level. It is opposite the angle, and \(80\) is adjacent. Use tangent.

\[ x = 80\tan 62^\circ \approx 150.5 \]

Add her eye height: \(150.5 + 5.5 = 156.0\).

The tower is about \(156.0\) feet tall.

Example 2: A lighthouse and a boat

The top of a lighthouse is \(120\) feet above the sea. The angle of depression to a boat is \(25^\circ\). How far is the boat from the base of the lighthouse? Round to the nearest tenth.

The angle of depression at the top equals the angle of elevation at the boat, \(25^\circ\). The height \(120\) is opposite that angle. The distance \(d\) is adjacent.

\[ \tan 25^\circ = \frac{120}{d} \quad\Rightarrow\quad d = \frac{120}{\tan 25^\circ} \approx 257.3 \]

The boat is about \(257.3\) feet from the base.

Example 3: An airplane landing

A plane is \(1500\) feet above the ground. The pilot sees the end of the runway at an angle of depression of \(3^\circ\). How far is the plane from the runway, along the ground? Round to the nearest foot. Then change the answer to miles.

\[ \tan 3^\circ = \frac{1500}{d} \quad\Rightarrow\quad d = \frac{1500}{\tan 3^\circ} \approx 28,622 \text{ feet} \]

One mile is \(5280\) feet. So \(d \approx 28,622 \div 5280 \approx 5.4\) miles.

Example 4: A drone

An operator stands \(120\) meters from the point on the ground directly under a drone. The angle of elevation to the drone is \(35^\circ\). Ignore the operator’s height. How high is the drone? How far is it from the operator in a straight line? Round to the nearest tenth.

  • Height: \(h = 120\tan 35^\circ \approx 84.0\) meters.
  • Straight-line distance \(L\): \(\cos 35^\circ = \dfrac{120}{L}\), so \(L = \dfrac{120}{\cos 35^\circ} \approx 146.5\) meters.

  1. Moses stands \(40\) feet from a tree. His eyes are \(5\) feet above the ground. The angle of elevation to the top of the tree is \(56^\circ\). How tall is the tree?

  2. A hiker stands on a cliff \(60\) feet high. The angle of depression to a boat is \(20^\circ\). How far is the boat from the base of the cliff?

  1. \(40\tan 56^\circ \approx 59.3\), and \(59.3 + 5 = 64.3\) feet.

  2. \(d = \dfrac{60}{\tan 20^\circ} \approx 164.8\) feet.

Example 5: Find the angle

Olena’s eyes are \(5\) feet above the ground. She stands \(50\) feet from a flagpole that is \(35\) feet tall. Find the angle of elevation to the top. Round to the nearest tenth.

The part of the pole above her eye level is \(35 - 5 = 30\) feet.

\[ \tan\theta = \frac{30}{50} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \theta = \tan^{-1}(0.6) \approx 31.0^\circ \]

Samir says: “The angle of elevation is \(28^\circ\).” He measured from the vertical in the picture of Section 6.6.1. What should it be?

The angle of elevation is measured from the horizontal. It is \(90^\circ - 28^\circ = 62^\circ\).

6.6.4 - Two Angles

Sometimes you see the same object from two places. Then you have two right triangles that share a side. Write an equation for each triangle and solve for the unknown.

Example 6: Moving closer

From point \(A\), the angle of elevation to the top of a tower is \(30^\circ\). After walking \(100\) feet toward the tower to point \(B\), the angle is \(45^\circ\). Find the height \(h\) of the tower. Round to the nearest tenth. (Ignore eye height.)

  • From \(B\): \(\tan 45^\circ = \dfrac{h}{x}\). Since \(\tan 45^\circ = 1\), \(h = x\).
  • From \(A\): \(\tan 30^\circ = \dfrac{h}{x + 100}\). So \(h = (x + 100)\tan 30^\circ\).
  • Replace \(h\) with \(x\): \(x = (x + 100)(0.5774)\). Then \(x - 0.5774x = 57.74\), so \(0.4226x = 57.74\) and \(x \approx 136.6\).

The tower is about \(136.6\) feet tall.

A clinometer measures an angle of elevation. Work with a partner. Tape a drinking straw along the flat edge of a protractor. Tie a string with a washer to the center hole. Look through the straw at the top of a tree or a flagpole. The string hangs straight down. Read the angle between the string and the \(90^\circ\) mark. That is the angle of elevation. Measure how far you stand from the object. Then find the height with tangent, and add your eye height.

Airplanes land along a path of about \(3^\circ\). That is a gentle slope. At \(1500\) feet of height, the plane is more than \(5\) miles from the runway.
