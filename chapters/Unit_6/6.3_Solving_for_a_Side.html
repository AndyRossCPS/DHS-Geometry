6.3 - Solving for a Side

How high does a \(24\)-foot ladder reach when it leans against a wall at \(75^\circ\)? You cannot hold a tape measure to the top. You can still find the answer.

The ladder is the hypotenuse. The angle is known. The height is the opposite side. One angle and one side are enough. In this lesson you use sine, cosine, and tangent to find a missing side.

sine, cosine, tangent, opposite side, adjacent side, hypotenuse

🔥 Warm Up

  1. Solve \(\dfrac{x}{12} = 0.6428\).

  2. Solve \(\dfrac{8}{x} = 0.9063\). Round to the nearest tenth.

  3. Find \(\sin 40^\circ\), \(\cos 40^\circ\), and \(\tan 40^\circ\) to four decimal places.

👥 Learn Together

6.3.1 - Choose the Ratio

To find a missing side, use these steps.

  1. Draw the triangle. Mark the angle you know.
  2. Label the sides opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse for that angle.
  3. Find the side you know and the side you want.
  4. Pick the ratio that uses exactly those two sides.
  5. Write the equation, solve, and round.
I know I want Use
opposite and hypotenuse (either one) the other one sin
adjacent and hypotenuse (either one) the other one cos
opposite and adjacent (either one) the other one tan

Say the two sides out loud. “Opposite and hypotenuse” is SOH, so use sine. “Adjacent and hypotenuse” is CAH, so use cosine. “Opposite and adjacent” is TOA, so use tangent.

6.3.2 - The Unknown Is on Top

When the side you want is on top of the fraction, multiply both sides by the number on the bottom.

Example 1: Opposite from the hypotenuse

Find \(x\). Round to the nearest tenth.

  • The known side, \(12\), is the hypotenuse. The wanted side, \(x\), is opposite the \(40^\circ\) angle.
  • Opposite and hypotenuse: use sine.

\[ \sin 40^\circ = \frac{x}{12} \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 12\sin 40^\circ \approx 7.7 \]

Example 2: Opposite from the adjacent

Find \(x\) to the nearest tenth. The side of length \(15\) is adjacent to the \(52^\circ\) angle. The side \(x\) is opposite it. Use tangent.

\[ \tan 52^\circ = \frac{x}{15} \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 15\tan 52^\circ \approx 19.2 \]

Example 3: Adjacent from the hypotenuse

Find \(x\) to the nearest tenth. The side \(x\) is adjacent to the \(28^\circ\) angle. The hypotenuse is \(20\). Use cosine.

\[ \cos 28^\circ = \frac{x}{20} \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 20\cos 28^\circ \approx 17.7 \]

  1. A right triangle has a \(23^\circ\) angle and a hypotenuse of \(18\). Find the side opposite the angle.

  2. A right triangle has a \(61^\circ\) angle. The adjacent side is \(9\). Find the opposite side.

  1. \(x = 18\sin 23^\circ \approx 7.0\)

  2. \(x = 9\tan 61^\circ \approx 16.2\)

6.3.3 - The Unknown Is on the Bottom

Sometimes the unknown is the denominator. Then you divide.

Example 4: The hypotenuse from the adjacent side

Find \(x\) to the nearest tenth. The side \(8\) is adjacent to the \(25^\circ\) angle. The side \(x\) is the hypotenuse. Use cosine.

\[ \cos 25^\circ = \frac{8}{x} \]

Multiply both sides by \(x\). Then divide both sides by \(\cos 25^\circ\):

\[ x \cdot \cos 25^\circ = 8 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = \frac{8}{\cos 25^\circ} \approx 8.8 \]

Do not write \(x = 8\cos 25^\circ\). That gives \(7.3\), and a leg cannot be longer than the hypotenuse. Check: the hypotenuse must be the longest side. If your answer for the hypotenuse is smaller than a leg, you multiplied when you should have divided.

Example 5: The adjacent side from the opposite side

Find \(x\) to the nearest tenth. The side \(9\) is opposite the \(34^\circ\) angle. The side \(x\) is adjacent. Use tangent.

\[ \tan 34^\circ = \frac{9}{x} \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = \frac{9}{\tan 34^\circ} \approx 13.3 \]

Example 6: The hypotenuse from the opposite side

Find \(x\) to the nearest tenth. The side \(14\) is opposite the \(63^\circ\) angle. The side \(x\) is the hypotenuse. Use sine.

\[ \sin 63^\circ = \frac{14}{x} \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = \frac{14}{\sin 63^\circ} \approx 15.7 \]

  1. A right triangle has a \(55^\circ\) angle. The adjacent side is \(10\). Find the hypotenuse.

  2. A right triangle has a \(47^\circ\) angle. The opposite side is \(6\). Find the adjacent side.

  3. A right triangle has a \(41^\circ\) angle. The opposite side is \(11\). Find the hypotenuse.

  1. \(x = \dfrac{10}{\cos 55^\circ} \approx 17.4\)

  2. \(x = \dfrac{6}{\tan 47^\circ} \approx 5.6\)

  3. \(x = \dfrac{11}{\sin 41^\circ} \approx 16.8\)

6.3.4 - Find All the Missing Sides

Example 7: Both legs

The hypotenuse is \(20\) and an acute angle is \(35^\circ\). Find \(x\) and \(y\) to the nearest tenth. Then check with the Pythagorean theorem.

  • \(x\) is opposite \(35^\circ\): \(x = 20\sin 35^\circ \approx 11.5\).
  • \(y\) is adjacent to \(35^\circ\): \(y = 20\cos 35^\circ \approx 16.4\).
  • Check: \(11.5^2 + 16.4^2 \approx 401.2\), and \(20^2 = 400\). The small difference comes from rounding.

If you use the exact calculator values instead of the rounded ones, the check is closer. Always use the numbers you were given when you can, and do not use rounded answers to find other sides.

6.3.5 - Real Problems

Draw the right triangle first. Label the angle and the sides you know. Answer in a sentence with units.

Example 8: The ladder

A \(24\)-foot ladder leans against a wall. It makes a \(75^\circ\) angle with the ground. How high up the wall does it reach? How far is the base from the wall? Round to the nearest tenth of a foot.

  • Height \(h\) is opposite \(75^\circ\): \(h = 24\sin 75^\circ \approx 23.2\) feet.
  • Base distance is adjacent: \(24\cos 75^\circ \approx 6.2\) feet.

The ladder reaches about \(23.2\) feet up the wall. Its base is about \(6.2\) feet from the wall.

Example 9: The guy wire

A guy wire holds up a cell tower. It is anchored \(30\) feet from the base of the tower. The wire makes a \(58^\circ\) angle with the ground. How tall is the tower where the wire is attached? How long is the wire? Round to the nearest tenth.

  • The \(30\) ft side is adjacent. The height \(h\) is opposite. Use tangent: \(h = 30\tan 58^\circ \approx 48.0\) feet.
  • The wire \(L\) is the hypotenuse. Use cosine: \(\cos 58^\circ = \dfrac{30}{L}\), so \(L = \dfrac{30}{\cos 58^\circ} \approx 56.6\) feet.

Example 10: A kite

A kite string is \(100\) feet long. It makes a \(52^\circ\) angle with the ground. Ignore the sag in the string. How high is the kite? Round to the nearest foot.

\(h = 100\sin 52^\circ \approx 78.8\). The kite is about \(79\) feet high.

Height: \(12\sin 38^\circ \approx 7.4\) feet. Ground distance: \(12\cos 38^\circ \approx 9.5\) feet.

Moses solves \(\cos 25^\circ = \dfrac{8}{x}\) and writes \(x = 8\cos 25^\circ \approx 7.3\). What is wrong?

He multiplied. The unknown is in the denominator, so he must divide: \(x = \dfrac{8}{\cos 25^\circ} \approx 8.8\). His answer is also impossible, because the hypotenuse \(x\) must be longer than the leg \(8\).
