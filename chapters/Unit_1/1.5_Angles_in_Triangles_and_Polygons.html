1.5 - Angles in Triangles and Polygons

A stop sign is a regular octagon. A honeycomb cell is a regular hexagon. A gazebo floor, a floor tile, and a soccer ball panel are all polygons. Each corner of these shapes is an angle.

Every triangle has angles that add to the same number. Because of that, we can find the angles in any polygon. In this lesson you learn the rules and use them to solve for missing angles.

triangle, scalene triangle, isosceles triangle, equilateral triangle, equiangular, exterior angle, polygon, regular polygon, convex polygon

1.5.1 - The Triangle Angle Sum

Cut the three corners off a paper triangle. Put the corners together at one point. They make a straight line.

The three angles of a triangle add to \(180^\circ\).

Paper can fool us, so here is the reason. Draw a line through \(B\) parallel to \(\overline{AC}\).

The angle marked \(1\) at \(B\) is equal to the angle marked \(1\) at \(A\). They are alternate interior angles. The angle marked \(3\) at \(B\) is equal to the angle marked \(3\) at \(C\). The three angles at \(B\) make a straight line, so \(1 + 2 + 3 = 180^\circ\).

Example 1: Find the third angle

A triangle has angles of \(48^\circ\) and \(67^\circ\). Find the third angle.

\[ 48 + 67 + x = 180 \quad\text{so}\quad 115 + x = 180 \quad\text{so}\quad x = 65 \]

A triangle has a right angle and a \(37^\circ\) angle. Find the third angle.

\(90 + 37 + x = 180\), so \(x = 53^\circ\).

Example 2: Solve for \(x\)

The angles of a triangle are \((2x)^\circ\), \((3x + 10)^\circ\), and \((x - 4)^\circ\). Find \(x\) and each angle.

\[ 2x + (3x + 10) + (x - 4) = 180 \quad\text{so}\quad 6x + 6 = 180 \quad\text{so}\quad x = 29 \]

The angles are \(2(29) = 58^\circ\), \(3(29) + 10 = 97^\circ\), and \(29 - 4 = 25^\circ\). Check: \(58 + 97 + 25 = 180\).

The angles of a triangle are \((x + 15)^\circ\), \((2x - 5)^\circ\), and \((3x + 2)^\circ\). Find \(x\) and each angle.

\(6x + 12 = 180\), so \(x = 28\). The angles are \(43^\circ\), \(51^\circ\), and \(86^\circ\).

1.5.2 - Classifying Triangles

We name a triangle two ways: by its sides and by its angles.

By sides Meaning
scalene no sides the same length
isosceles at least two sides the same length
equilateral all three sides the same length
By angles Meaning
acute all three angles less than \(90^\circ\)
right one \(90^\circ\) angle
obtuse one angle more than \(90^\circ\)
equiangular all three angles the same (\(60^\circ\) each)

In an isosceles triangle, the two angles across from the equal sides are equal. They are the base angles.

Example 3: Name it two ways

  1. A triangle has sides \(4\), \(4\), and \(4\) and angles \(60^\circ\), \(60^\circ\), \(60^\circ\).

    It is equilateral and equiangular.

  2. A triangle has angles \(90^\circ\), \(45^\circ\), and \(45^\circ\).

    It has a \(90^\circ\) angle, so it is right. It has two equal angles, so it is isosceles.

Example 4: Base angles

The tick marks show \(AB = BC\). The angle at \(B\) is \(40^\circ\). Find the base angles.

The base angles are equal. The three angles add to \(180\), so the two base angles add to \(180 - 40 = 140\). Each one is \(140 \div 2 = 70^\circ\).

An isosceles triangle has a base angle of \(55^\circ\). Find the angle at the top.

The base angles are \(55^\circ\) and \(55^\circ\). \(180 - 55 - 55 = 70^\circ\).

Example 5: An isosceles triangle with \(x\)

The tick marks show \(AB = BC\). Find \(x\) and the perimeter.

\[ 3x + 1 = 2x + 9 \quad\text{so}\quad x = 8 \]

Each equal side is \(3(8) + 1 = 25\). The perimeter is \(25 + 25 + 12 = 62\).

The equal sides of an isosceles triangle are \((5x - 4)\) and \((3x + 10)\). The third side is \(20\). Find \(x\) and the perimeter.

\(5x - 4 = 3x + 10\), so \(x = 7\). Each equal side is \(31\). The perimeter is \(31 + 31 + 20 = 82\).

1.5.3 - Exterior Angles

Extend one side of a triangle. The angle outside the triangle, next to an inside angle, is an exterior angle. The two inside angles far from it are the remote interior angles.

An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of its two remote interior angles.

Why? The exterior angle and its inside neighbor are a linear pair, so they add to \(180^\circ\). The three inside angles also add to \(180^\circ\). So the exterior angle equals the other two inside angles together.

Example 6: Find an exterior angle

In the figure, the remote angles are \(50^\circ\) and \(65^\circ\). The exterior angle is \(50 + 65 = 115^\circ\).

An exterior angle is \(130^\circ\). One remote angle is \(80^\circ\). Find the other remote angle.

\(130 - 80 = 50^\circ\).

Example 7: Solve for \(x\)

Find \(x\) and the exterior angle.

\[ 4x - 6 = (x + 30) + (x + 36) \quad\text{so}\quad 4x - 6 = 2x + 66 \quad\text{so}\quad x = 36 \]

The exterior angle is \(4(36) - 6 = 138^\circ\). The remote angles are \(66^\circ\) and \(72^\circ\).

An exterior angle is \((7x - 3)^\circ\). The remote angles are \((2x + 22)^\circ\) and \((3x + 5)^\circ\). Find \(x\) and the exterior angle.

\(7x - 3 = 5x + 27\), so \(x = 15\). The exterior angle is \(102^\circ\).

1.5.4 - Polygons

A polygon is a closed figure made of segments. We name a polygon by its number of sides.

Sides Name Sides Name
3 triangle 7 heptagon
4 quadrilateral 8 octagon
5 pentagon 9 nonagon
6 hexagon 10 decagon

A polygon with \(n\) sides is an \(n\)-gon. A polygon is convex if no corner points inward (see the figure at the top of the lesson). A regular polygon has all sides equal and all angles equal.

The angle sum. Pick one corner. Draw all the diagonals from it. They cut the polygon into triangles.

A hexagon makes \(4\) triangles, and each triangle has \(180^\circ\). So the angles of a hexagon add to \(4 \cdot 180 = 720^\circ\).

A polygon with \(n\) sides makes \(n - 2\) triangles. The interior angles add to \[ (n - 2) \cdot 180^\circ. \]

Example 8: Angle sum

Find the angle sum of a pentagon and of an octagon.

Pentagon: \((5 - 2) \cdot 180 = 540^\circ\).

Octagon: \((8 - 2) \cdot 180 = 1080^\circ\).

Find the angle sum of a hexagon. Check it with the figure above.

\((6 - 2) \cdot 180 = 720^\circ\). It matches the \(4\) triangles.

Example 9: One angle of a regular polygon

In a regular polygon, all angles are equal. Divide the sum by \(n\).

  1. Regular pentagon: \(540 \div 5 = 108^\circ\).

  2. Regular decagon: \((10 - 2) \cdot 180 = 1440\), and \(1440 \div 10 = 144^\circ\).

Find one angle of a regular hexagon.

\(720 \div 6 = 120^\circ\).

Exterior angles. Walk around a polygon. At each corner you turn by the exterior angle. After one trip around you have turned one full circle, \(360^\circ\).

The exterior angles of any convex polygon add to \(360^\circ\).

For a regular polygon with \(n\) sides, each exterior angle is \(360 \div n\). Each interior angle is \(180\) minus the exterior angle.

Example 10: Find the number of sides

  1. The angles of a polygon add to \(1260^\circ\). Find \(n\).

    \((n - 2) \cdot 180 = 1260\), so \(n - 2 = 7\) and \(n = 9\).

  2. Each angle of a regular polygon is \(150^\circ\). Find \(n\).

    The exterior angle is \(180 - 150 = 30^\circ\). So \(n = 360 \div 30 = 12\).

The angles of a polygon add to \(1620^\circ\). Find \(n\).

\(n - 2 = 9\), so \(n = 11\).

Example 11: Solve for \(x\)

A quadrilateral has angles \(x^\circ\), \((2x)^\circ\), \((x + 50)^\circ\), and \(90^\circ\). Find \(x\) and each angle.

The sum for a quadrilateral is \((4 - 2) \cdot 180 = 360\).

\[ x + 2x + (x + 50) + 90 = 360 \quad\text{so}\quad 4x + 140 = 360 \quad\text{so}\quad x = 55 \]

The angles are \(55^\circ\), \(110^\circ\), \(105^\circ\), and \(90^\circ\).

A quadrilateral has angles \(x^\circ\), \((x + 20)^\circ\), \((2x)^\circ\), and \((x + 40)^\circ\). Find \(x\) and each angle.

\(5x + 60 = 360\), so \(x = 60\). The angles are \(60^\circ\), \(80^\circ\), \(120^\circ\), and \(100^\circ\).

✍️ Practice On Your Own

The triangle angle sum.

  1. A triangle has angles of \(35^\circ\) and \(80^\circ\). Find the third angle.

  2. A triangle has angles of \(112^\circ\) and \(28^\circ\). Find the third angle.

  3. A right triangle has an acute angle of \(54^\circ\). Find the other acute angle.

  4. The angles of a triangle are \((2x + 4)^\circ\), \((3x - 9)^\circ\), and \((x + 5)^\circ\). Find \(x\) and each angle.

  5. The angles of a triangle are \(x^\circ\), \((2x)^\circ\), and \((3x)^\circ\). Find each angle. Then classify the triangle by its angles.

Classify triangles.

  1. Sides \(6\), \(6\), and \(9\). Classify by sides.

  2. Sides \(5\), \(8\), and \(10\). Classify by sides.

  3. Angles \(20^\circ\), \(40^\circ\), and \(120^\circ\). Classify by angles.

  4. Angles \(50^\circ\), \(60^\circ\), and \(70^\circ\). Classify by angles.

  5. An isosceles triangle has a top angle of \(80^\circ\). Find the base angles.

  6. An isosceles triangle has a base angle of \(35^\circ\). Find the top angle.

  7. An isosceles triangle has equal sides \((4x - 3)\) and \((2x + 11)\). The third side is \(14\). Find \(x\) and the perimeter.

Exterior angles.

  1. The remote angles are \(40^\circ\) and \(75^\circ\). Find the exterior angle.

  2. An exterior angle is \(140^\circ\). One remote angle is \(90^\circ\). Find the other remote angle.

  3. An exterior angle is \((7x - 10)^\circ\). The remote angles are \((3x + 2)^\circ\) and \((2x + 12)^\circ\). Find \(x\) and the three angles.

  4. A triangle has angles \(50^\circ\), \(60^\circ\), and \(70^\circ\). Kofi says the exterior angle at the \(70^\circ\) corner is \(50 + 60 + 70 = 180^\circ\). What did he do wrong? Find the correct exterior angle.

Polygons.

  1. Name a polygon with 6 sides. Name a polygon with 9 sides.

  2. Find the angle sum of a heptagon.

  3. Find the angle sum of a decagon.

  4. Find the angle sum of a regular nonagon. Then find one angle.

  5. A quadrilateral has angles \(85^\circ\), \(95^\circ\), \(110^\circ\), and \(x^\circ\). Find \(x\).

  6. Each angle of a regular polygon is \(156^\circ\). Find the number of sides.

  7. Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is \(40^\circ\). Find the number of sides.

Real problems.

  1. A stop sign is a regular octagon. Find each interior angle and each exterior angle.

  2. Esperanza wants to cover a floor with regular pentagon tiles. The corners must meet at a point with no gap. Will it work? (Hint: the angles around a point add to \(360^\circ\).)

What does the figure show?

Challenge

  1. Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 5 times its exterior angle. Find the number of sides.

  2. Use the interior angle sum to explain why the exterior angles of a polygon add to \(360^\circ\). (Hint: at each corner, interior plus exterior is \(180^\circ\).)
