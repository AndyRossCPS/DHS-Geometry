👥 Learn Together

3.1 - Translations

Example 1: Translate \(P(-3, 4)\) by \((x + 5, y - 6)\). Then find the rule that moves \(A(2, -1)\) to \(A'(-4, 3)\).

\(P' = (2, -2)\). Change in \(x\): \(-4 - 2 = -6\). Change in \(y\): \(3 - (-1) = 4\). Rule: \((x - 6, y + 4)\).

3.2 - Reflections

Example 2: Reflect \((-4, 3)\) over the \(y\)-axis and over \(y = x\). Find the line of reflection that maps \(A(0, 2)\) to \(A'(6, 2)\).

\(y\)-axis: \((4, 3)\). \(y = x\): \((3, -4)\). The midpoint of \(\overline{AA'}\) is \((3, 2)\) and \(\overline{AA'}\) is horizontal, so the line is \(x = 3\).

3.3 - Rotations

Example 3: Rotate \((3, -2)\) by \(90^\circ\) and by \(180^\circ\) counterclockwise about the origin. Find the rotation that maps \((-1, -5)\) to \((-5, 1)\).

\(90^\circ\): \((-y, x) = (2, 3)\). \(180^\circ\): \((-3, 2)\). For \((-1, -5) \rightarrow (-5, 1)\) the rule \((y, -x)\) works, so it is \(270^\circ\) counterclockwise (\(90^\circ\) clockwise).

3.4 - Dilations

Example 4: Dilate \((6, -9)\) with \(k = \dfrac{1}{3}\) about the origin. A side of 8 becomes 20. Find \(k\). Dilate \(B(5, 3)\) with \(k = 2\) about \((1, 1)\).

\((2, -3)\). \(k = \dfrac{20}{8} = \dfrac{5}{2}\). \(B\): \((4, 2) \rightarrow (8, 4) \rightarrow (9, 5)\).

3.5 - Sequences of Transformations and Symmetry

Example 5: Do “translate by \((x + 2, y)\), then reflect over the \(y\)-axis” on \(P(1, 3)\). Do the moves in the other order. Then give the order of rotational symmetry and the smallest angle for a regular octagon.

First order: \((3, 3) \rightarrow (-3, 3)\). Other order: \((-1, 3) \rightarrow (1, 3)\). Different. A regular octagon has order 8 and smallest angle \(\dfrac{360^\circ}{8} = 45^\circ\).

✍️ Practice On Your Own

3.1 - I can translate a figure, write a translation rule, and check it with distance and slope.

  1. Apply \((x - 6, y + 3)\) to \((4, -5)\).

  2. \(A(-3, 2)\) moves to \(A'(1, -4)\). Write the rule and say it in words.

  3. Triangle \(RST\) has \(R(0, 0)\), \(S(4, 0)\), \(T(0, 3)\). Translate it by \((x + 2, y + 5)\). Give the image coordinates.

  4. Show that \(ST = S'T'\) and that the slopes are equal for the triangle in problem 3.

  5. The image of \((3a, a + 2)\) under \((x - 5, y + 1)\) is \((4, 6)\). Find \(a\).

3.2 - I can reflect a figure over a line and find the line of reflection.

  1. Reflect \((-5, 2)\) over the \(x\)-axis.

  2. Reflect \((3, -6)\) over the line \(y = -x\).

  3. Reflect \((1, 4)\) over the line \(x = -2\).

  4. \(A(-2, 3)\) is reflected to \(A'(4, 3)\). Find the line of reflection.

  5. Triangle \(ABC\) has \(A(1, 1)\), \(B(5, 2)\), \(C(2, 4)\). Reflect it over the \(x\)-axis. Does the orientation stay the same?

3.3 - I can rotate a figure about the origin and find a rotation.

  1. Rotate \((-4, 1)\) by \(90^\circ\) counterclockwise about the origin.

  2. Rotate \((2, -7)\) by \(180^\circ\) about the origin.

  3. Rotate \((5, 3)\) by \(90^\circ\) clockwise about the origin.

  4. \((4, -2) \rightarrow (2, 4)\) about the origin. Find the rotation.

  5. \(A(3, 4)\) rotates \(90^\circ\) counterclockwise to \(A'(-4, 3)\). Show \(OA = OA'\) and \(\overline{OA} \perp \overline{OA'}\).

3.4 - I can dilate a figure and find a scale factor.

  1. Dilate \((-4, 6)\) with \(k = \dfrac{1}{2}\) about the origin.

  2. Dilate \((3, -2)\) with \(k = 4\) about the origin.

  3. \(A(2, 6) \rightarrow A'(5, 15)\) about the origin. Find \(k\).

  4. Dilate \((4, 4)\) with \(k = 3\) about the center \((2, 3)\).

  5. A triangle with sides 6, 8, 10 is dilated with \(k = 1.5\). Find the new sides. What happens to the angles?

3.5 - I can do a sequence of transformations and find symmetry.

  1. Translate \((2, 3)\) by \((x + 3, y - 2)\). Then reflect over the \(x\)-axis.

  2. Reflect over the \(y\)-axis, then translate by \((x + 4, y + 1)\). Write one rule.

  3. Use \(P(1, 2)\). Show that “rotate \(90^\circ\) counterclockwise, then translate by \((x + 3, y)\)” is not the same as “translate first, then rotate”.

  4. Find the lines of symmetry, the order of rotational symmetry, and the smallest angle for a regular hexagon.

  5. Find the lines of symmetry and the order of rotational symmetry of the letter S.

Test practice

  1. Point \(Q(-2, 5)\) is translated by \((x + 4, y - 7)\). What are the coordinates of \(Q'\)?

    A. \((2, -2)\)
    B. \((-6, 12)\)
    C. \((2, 12)\)
    D. \((-6, -2)\)

  2. What are the coordinates of the image of \((6, -1)\) after a reflection over the line \(y = x\)?

    A. \((-1, 6)\)
    B. \((1, -6)\)
    C. \((6, 1)\)
    D. \((-6, -1)\)

  3. What are the coordinates of the image of \((-3, -2)\) after a rotation of \(270^\circ\) counterclockwise about the origin?

    A. \((-2, 3)\)
    B. \((2, -3)\)
    C. \((3, 2)\)
    D. \((2, 3)\)

  4. A dilation about the origin has scale factor \(\dfrac{2}{3}\). What is the image of \((9, -6)\)?

    A. \((6, -4)\)
    B. \((13.5, -9)\)
    C. \((6, 4)\)
    D. \((-6, 4)\)

  5. A transformation keeps all lengths and angles but reverses the orientation of a figure. Which transformation is it?

    A. a reflection
    B. a translation
    C. a rotation
    D. a dilation

  6. A regular pentagon has rotational symmetry. What is the smallest angle of rotation that maps it onto itself?

    A. \(72^\circ\)
    B. \(60^\circ\)
    C. \(90^\circ\)
    D. \(108^\circ\)

  7. Which of these is NOT a rigid motion?

    A. a dilation with scale factor 3
    B. a rotation of \(90^\circ\)
    C. a reflection over the \(x\)-axis
    D. a translation by \((x + 2, y)\)
