7.1 - Circumference and Arc Length

A bike wheel is 26 inches across. Every time the wheel turns once, the bike rolls forward. How far does it go?

The answer is the distance around the wheel. In this lesson you learn the parts of a circle and how to find the distance around it. You also learn how to find the length of just one piece of the edge.

circle, center of a circle, radius, diameter, chord, circumference, pi, central angle, arc, minor arc, major arc, semicircle, arc measure, arc length

7.1.1 - Parts of a Circle

A circle is all the points in a plane that are the same distance from one point. That point is the center. We name a circle by its center. Circle \(P\) is written \(\odot P\).

Part What it is In the figure
radius a segment from the center to a point on the circle \(\overline{PA}\)
diameter a chord that goes through the center \(\overline{BC}\)
chord a segment with both endpoints on the circle \(\overline{EF}\)
arc part of the circle between two points \(\overset{\frown}{AD}\)
central angle an angle with its vertex at the center \(\angle APD\)

All radii of one circle have the same length. A diameter is two radii in a line, so \[ d = 2r \qquad\text{and}\qquad r = \frac{d}{2}. \] The diameter is the longest chord of a circle.

An arc has a name too.

  • A minor arc is less than half of the circle. We name it with its two endpoints: \(\overset{\frown}{AD}\).
  • A semicircle is exactly half of the circle. We name it with three letters: \(\overset{\frown}{BAC}\).
  • A major arc is more than half of the circle. We name it with three letters: \(\overset{\frown}{EAF}\) is the long way around from \(E\) to \(F\) through \(A\).

Example 1: Name the parts

  1. Name a radius.

    \(\overline{OB}\). (Also \(\overline{OA}\), \(\overline{OC}\), and \(\overline{OD}\).)

  2. Name a diameter.

    \(\overline{AC}\). It is a chord that goes through the center \(O\).

  3. Name a chord that is not a diameter.

    \(\overline{BD}\). Both endpoints are on the circle, but it does not go through \(O\).

  4. Name a semicircle.

    \(\overset{\frown}{ABC}\). Points \(A\) and \(C\) are the ends of a diameter, so the arc from \(A\) to \(C\) through \(B\) is half of the circle.

  5. Name a central angle.

    \(\angle BOC\). Its vertex is the center \(O\).

  1. Name another radius that is part of the diameter.

  2. Name the semicircle that goes through \(D\).

  1. \(\overline{OA}\) or \(\overline{OC}\)

  2. \(\overset{\frown}{ADC}\)

Example 2: Radius and diameter

  1. The diameter is \(18\) inches. Find the radius.

    \(r = \dfrac{18}{2} = 9\) inches.

  2. The radius is \(7.5\) feet. Find the diameter.

    \(d = 2(7.5) = 15\) feet.

  3. A chord goes through the center and is \(26\) centimeters long. Find the radius.

    A chord through the center is a diameter. So \(d = 26\) and \(r = 13\) centimeters.

  1. \(d = 11\) meters. Find \(r\).

  2. \(r = 4.2\) inches. Find \(d\).

  1. \(r = 5.5\) meters

  2. \(d = 8.4\) inches

7.1.2 - Circumference and Pi

The distance around a circle is its circumference. Measure some round things. Wrap a string around each one, then measure the string. Divide the circumference \(C\) by the diameter \(d\).

Object \(C\) \(d\) \(C \div d\)
bike tire \(207\) cm \(66\) cm \(3.14\)
dinner plate \(81.7\) cm \(26\) cm \(3.14\)
trash can lid \(188\) cm \(60\) cm \(3.13\)
coin \(7.5\) cm \(2.4\) cm \(3.13\)
soup can \(23.9\) cm \(7.6\) cm \(3.14\)

Every answer is close to \(3.14\). The small differences come from measuring. For every circle, \(C \div d\) is the same number. We call it pi and write it \(\pi\). It is about \(3.14159\). It goes on forever without a pattern.

\[ C = \pi d \qquad\text{or}\qquad C = 2\pi r \] An answer like \(12\pi\) is exact. For a decimal, use the \(\pi\) key on your calculator.

Example 3: Circumference from the diameter

A round fountain has a diameter of \(12\) feet. Find its circumference.

\[ C = \pi d = \pi(12) = 12\pi \text{ feet} \]

On a calculator, \(12\pi \approx 37.70\). The circumference is \(12\pi\) feet, or about \(37.70\) feet.

Example 4: Circumference from the radius

A circle has a radius of \(5.5\) inches. Find its circumference.

\[ C = 2\pi r = 2\pi(5.5) = 11\pi \approx 34.56 \text{ inches} \]

  1. \(d = 20\) feet

  2. \(r = 3.5\) centimeters

  1. \(C = 20\pi \approx 62.83\) feet

  2. \(C = 2\pi(3.5) = 7\pi \approx 21.99\) centimeters

Example 5: Find the radius from the circumference

  1. A circle has a circumference of \(28\pi\) centimeters. Find the radius.

    \(2\pi r = 28\pi\). Divide both sides by \(2\pi\). Then \(r = 14\) centimeters.

  2. A circle has a circumference of \(50\) inches. Find the radius. Round to the nearest tenth.

    \(2\pi r = 50\), so \(r = \dfrac{50}{2\pi} = \dfrac{25}{\pi} \approx 8.0\) inches.

  1. \(C = 36\pi\) feet

  2. \(C = 90\) centimeters. Round to the nearest tenth.

  1. \(r = 18\) feet

  2. \(r = \dfrac{90}{2\pi} = \dfrac{45}{\pi} \approx 14.3\) centimeters

Example 6: The bike wheel

How far does a wheel with a diameter of \(26\) inches roll in one turn? How many turns make one mile? Round to the nearest tenth.

One turn is one circumference:

\[ C = \pi(26) = 26\pi \approx 81.7 \text{ inches}. \]

One mile is \(5{,}280 \times 12 = 63{,}360\) inches. Divide:

\[ 63{,}360 \div 81.68 \approx 775.7 \text{ turns}. \]

The wheel rolls about \(81.7\) inches in one turn. It turns about \(775.7\) times in one mile.

  1. How far does it roll in one turn? Give the exact answer.

  2. How far does it roll in 5 turns? Round to the nearest tenth.

  1. \(20\pi\) inches

  2. \(5 \cdot 20\pi = 100\pi \approx 314.2\) inches

7.1.3 - Arc Measure and Arc Length

A central angle cuts a piece out of the edge of the circle. We can measure that piece in two different ways.

  • Arc measure tells how much of a turn the arc is. It is in degrees. It equals the central angle: \(m\overset{\frown}{AB} = 70^\circ\).
  • Arc length tells how far it is along the arc. It is a distance, in inches, feet, or meters.

The arc is the fraction \(\dfrac{\text{central angle}}{360^\circ}\) of the whole circle. So the arc length is that fraction of the circumference.

\[ \text{arc length} = \frac{\text{central angle}}{360^\circ} \cdot 2\pi r \]

A semicircle measures \(180^\circ\). A major arc measures \(360^\circ\) minus its minor arc.

Example 7: Arc measure

Use the figure. \(m\angle AOB = 70^\circ\).

  1. Find \(m\overset{\frown}{AB}\).

    The arc measure equals the central angle. \(m\overset{\frown}{AB} = 70^\circ\).

  2. Find \(m\overset{\frown}{ACB}\).

    \(\overset{\frown}{ACB}\) is the major arc. \(360^\circ - 70^\circ = 290^\circ\).

  1. Find the measure of its minor arc.

  2. Find the measure of the matching major arc.

  1. \(125^\circ\)

  2. \(360^\circ - 125^\circ = 235^\circ\)

Example 8: Arc length

In the figure, the radius is \(9\) feet. Find the length of \(\overset{\frown}{AB}\). Give the exact answer and a decimal rounded to the nearest hundredth.

\[ \text{arc length} = \frac{70}{360} \cdot 2\pi(9) = \frac{7}{36} \cdot 18\pi = 3.5\pi \]

The arc is \(3.5\pi\) feet, or about \(10.99\) feet.

\(r = 15\) inches and the central angle is \(48^\circ\). Give the exact answer and a decimal rounded to the nearest hundredth.

\(\dfrac{48}{360} \cdot 2\pi(15) = \dfrac{2}{15} \cdot 30\pi = 4\pi \approx 12.57\) inches

Example 9: The same degrees, a different length

Two circles share a center. The inner circle has radius \(3\) and the outer circle has radius \(6\). Both arcs have a \(60^\circ\) central angle. Find each arc length.

  • Inner arc \(\overset{\frown}{AB}\): \(\dfrac{60}{360} \cdot 2\pi(3) = \dfrac{1}{6} \cdot 6\pi = \pi \approx 3.14\).
  • Outer arc \(\overset{\frown}{CD}\): \(\dfrac{60}{360} \cdot 2\pi(6) = \dfrac{1}{6} \cdot 12\pi = 2\pi \approx 6.28\).

The two arcs both measure \(60^\circ\). But the outer arc is twice as long. Arc measure says how much of the circle. Arc length says how far. Two arcs with the same measure have the same length only in circles with the same radius.

7.1.4 - Solving with Arcs

The arcs that go all the way around a circle add up to \(360^\circ\).

Example 10: Solve for \(x\)

The four central angles are marked with arc measures. Find \(x\) and each arc.

\[ 2x + (3x + 10) + (x + 20) + (4x - 10) = 360 \]

\[ 10x + 20 = 360 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 10x = 340 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 34. \]

The arcs are \(2(34) = 68^\circ\), \(3(34) + 10 = 112^\circ\), \(34 + 20 = 54^\circ\), and \(4(34) - 10 = 126^\circ\).

Check: \(68 + 112 + 54 + 126 = 360\).

The arcs measure \(x^\circ\), \((2x + 20)^\circ\), \((3x - 10)^\circ\), and \((4x)^\circ\). Find \(x\) and each arc.

\(x + (2x + 20) + (3x - 10) + 4x = 360\), so \(10x + 10 = 360\) and \(x = 35\). The arcs are \(35^\circ\), \(90^\circ\), \(95^\circ\), and \(140^\circ\).

Example 11: Find the radius from an arc length

  1. An arc of \(150^\circ\) has length \(5\pi\) centimeters. Find the radius.

\[ \frac{150}{360} \cdot 2\pi r = 5\pi \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{5}{6}\pi r = 5\pi \quad\Rightarrow\quad r = 6. \]

The radius is \(6\) centimeters.

  1. An arc of \(90^\circ\) is \(20\) feet long. Find the radius. Round to the nearest hundredth.

\[ \frac{90}{360} \cdot 2\pi r = 20 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{\pi r}{2} = 20 \quad\Rightarrow\quad r = \frac{40}{\pi} \approx 12.73. \]

The radius is about \(12.73\) feet.

  1. An arc of \(120^\circ\) has length \(8\pi\) meters.

  2. An arc of \(60^\circ\) is \(15\) inches long. Round to the nearest hundredth.

  1. \(\dfrac{1}{3}(2\pi r) = 8\pi\), so \(r = 12\) meters.

  2. \(\dfrac{1}{6}(2\pi r) = 15\), so \(\dfrac{\pi r}{3} = 15\) and \(r = \dfrac{45}{\pi} \approx 14.32\) inches.

Example 12: A running track

Each end of a track is a semicircle with radius \(36.5\) meters. The two straight sides are \(84.4\) meters each. How long is one lap? Round to the nearest tenth.

The two ends together make one full circle:

\[ 2\pi(36.5) = 73\pi \approx 229.3 \text{ meters}. \]

Add the two straight sides: \(229.3 + 2(84.4) = 398.1\).

One lap is about \(398.1\) meters.
